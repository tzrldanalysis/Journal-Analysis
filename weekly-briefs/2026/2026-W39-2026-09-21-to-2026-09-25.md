## 1. The Week in One Paragraph

The thing that wasn't visible Monday morning is that the market has stopped waiting for AI-capex financing to get repriced. On Monday, both papers were still writing about fragility in the abstract — FT's $300bn Big Tech guarantee Big Read, WSJ's neutral-rate re-rating story. By Friday, SoftBank had actually priced the largest junk-bond deal on record (up to 9.75% yields, funding OpenAI's Stargate buildout) and watched it get oversubscribed anyway, on the same day the 10-year Treasury sat at 5.163%, the MOVE index hit its highest level since spring 2025, and the 30-year mortgage crossed 7%. The warnings didn't stop the financing; the financing happened anyway, at scale, while credit spreads stayed tight. Layered on top, the week converted a tactical Hormuz-routing story into an explicit alliance-reliability crisis — Saudi Arabia's frustration with US non-intervention against the Houthis (Thursday, sourced to a named ex-CENTCOM official) and Belgium's leaked internal memo calling the US "no longer" reliable (Friday) are different allies making structurally the same complaint, in the same week Washington staged a warm Trump-Xi summit that both papers' own reporting undercut same-day (Chinese drone/missile shipments to Iran, Xi's "coexist" language). Nothing broke this week. But two things that were being treated as contained risks — AI-credit leverage and alliance confidence — are visibly no longer contained, and neither paper's Friday edition treats either as resolved.
