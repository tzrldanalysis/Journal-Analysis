## 1. The Week in One Paragraph

On Monday the AI-capex risk was framed as a political and equity-valuation problem — blue-state data-centre moratoria, a $2.5tn Nasdaq wipeout, and a CEO scandal at Nvidia (WSJ, Mon 11/9; FT, Mon 11/9). By Friday it had become a financing-plumbing and rates problem that equity prices had largely stopped reflecting: Jensen Huang resigned, taking with him the personal deal machine behind $124bn of Nvidia equity stakes and more than $60bn of credit guarantees (FT, Fri 11/13); CoreWeave's CEO conceded the unsecured bond market is shut to the company at ~15% yields (WSJ, Thu 11/12); Barclays, HSBC and JPMorgan were buying credit insurance on data-centre loans as they "stretch internal limits" (FT, Thu 11/12); investment-grade funds were hitting 1–2% single-issuer caps on Amazon, Oracle, Meta and SpaceX (FT, Fri 11/13); and the Fed moved from "no more cuts" (WSJ, Mon 11/9) to an openly discussed 50bp December hike on 3.7% annualised core CPI (WSJ, Thu 11/12), with the 10-year reaching 5.10% (FT, Fri 11/13). The equity tape spent the same five days recovering nearly all of the rout (Nasdaq within 1% of erasing it, WSJ, Thu 11/12). What is now true that wasn't visible Monday: AI equity and AI credit are pricing two different worlds, and the cost of capital for the debt-funded half of the buildout (~$3tn of Goldman's $8.5tn 2026–31 capex forecast, FT, Fri 11/13) is rising at the same moment its central dealmaker has left.

## 2. Theme Arcs

### Arc 1 — AI stress migrated from equity to credit, and from issuers to intermediaries: escalating

- **Mon:** The only credit read was a non-reaction — ICE BofA HY 100 at 282bp, inside its 183–298bp range, despite VIX +32% to 25.64; the brief flagged "credit-market reaction to the AI selloff is simply missing" as an absent story (WSJ, Mon 11/9).
- **Tue:** First hard credit data. SoftBank's loan-to-value on pledged Arm shares rose to 18–22% (from 13% in June) against a 25% ceiling, Arm and other listed holdings were down ~60% since June, five-year CDS "increased sharply," and the $50bn SB Energy listing was at risk of delay (FT, Tue 11/10). WSJ the same day flagged the CoreWeave bond/private-credit angle as missing (WSJ, Tue 11/10).
- **Wed:** Spread widening described as "concentrated in names tied to AI capital expenditure financing rather than broad-based," with a SoftBank turbine-related writedown (WSJ, Wed 11/11 — thin edition, no spread figures).
- **Thu:** Issuer-level confirmation. CoreWeave beat Q3 and rallied 25%, but its 2032 9.625% bond yields ~15%; the CEO said it "will not use the unsecured debt market… at current yields"; $39bn of 2026 capex now rests on ~$9bn of customer prepayments over three months plus vendor financing; landlords want investment-grade rental backstops; Moody's flagged force-majeure risk (WSJ, Thu 11/12). FT moved one layer up: banks buying credit insurance, the CFTC postponing AI-compute futures to at least early 2027 as too "opaque and concentrated," Oklo's financing partners walking from land options (FT, Thu 11/12).
- **Fri:** System-level confirmation. IG portfolios at single-issuer caps; Goldman's $8.5tn capex forecast, ~35% debt-funded; Pimco's Karoui: "The concentration issue is real"; Nvidia's guarantee book (up to $105bn behind the Ohio campus, $20bn of leases it plans to sublet, 200+ start-up credit lines) left without its architect (FT, Fri 11/13).
- **Status: escalating.** The arc moved from "why hasn't credit moved?" (Mon) to issuer distress (Thu) to bank and IG-portfolio capacity limits (Fri). Monday's WSJ absent story became the week's dominant finding — the clearest case this week of the daily analysis spotting something before the papers built a story around it.

### Arc 2 — Nvidia went from a CEO scandal to a leadership vacuum with counterparty consequences: leadership question resolved, financing consequences escalating

- **Mon:** Huang admitted an affair with Megaspeed's Alice Huang, suspected of Chinese-intelligence ties; the board was investigating but saw "no evidence" of compromise; NVDA closed the prior week at $186.64 (WSJ, Mon 11/9). FT noted a Congressional pledge to investigate (FT, Mon 11/9).
- **Tue:** Stock fell again — $178.67, -4.3% (WSJ, Tue 11/10) / $171.87, -4.70% (FT, Tue 11/10). The papers report different closes for the same session; the FT figure is the more conservative. FT View and Lex turned it into a governance indictment: no chair, 18-year average director tenure, 60+ direct reports, ~7% of the S&P 500 (FT, Tue 11/10). FT also sourced the Alibaba-linked Megaspeed loan routed through Singapore and Malaysia.
- **Wed:** Framed as feeding chip-export-curb legislation, with Wachtell reportedly involved; NVDA $168.89 (WSJ, Wed 11/11).
- **Thu:** Board reportedly preparing to oust Huang over a second affair (Kimberly Powell, Nvidia healthcare-unit executive). Multiple DOJ investigations were under way and Warren and Coons demanded document preservation. The stock rose 2.9% intraday, then fell ~8% after hours (WSJ, Thu 11/12). FT reported Megaspeed's Nvidia billings rose from $33mn (2023) to as much as $5bn (FT, Thu 11/12).
- **Fri:** Huang resigned. Colette Kress is interim CEO and Harvey Jones the board's first-ever chair. The stock fell as much as 14% intraday and was down ~5% by midday, having lost more than $1.9tn of market value since its October peak. CoreWeave fell 7%, and a cloud executive expects Nvidia's dealmaking to "mostly come to a halt until the uproar around Huang was resolved" (FT, Fri 11/13).
- **Status:** The leadership question escalated every day and resolved on Friday. The open questions are now the guarantee/covenant book and export-control enforcement — Singapore says the DOJ/FBI has shared only "limited information" since 2025 (FT, Fri 11/13).

## 3. Thesis Tracker

**Leading finding — two priors broke outright within the week.**

**Prior:** Founder-concentrated governance at hypergrowth tech firms is a tolerable feature, not a systemic vulnerability (first flagged FT, Tue 11/10).
**Trajectory: broke outright.** The Wednesday fragility was triggered on Thursday: a second affair, DOJ investigations, and a board reportedly preparing an ouster (WSJ, Thu 11/12). Huang resigned and Nvidia installed its first-ever chair on Friday (FT, Fri 11/13). The board had already added "financial, counterparty and execution risk" as a 10-Q risk factor in August — so the company had written down this risk before the market priced it.

**Prior:** The Fed's tightening cycle is over / gradual 25bp cadence / developed-market central banks are on a glide path to cuts (three variants: WSJ, Wed 11/11; WSJ, Thu 11/12; FT, Fri 11/13).
**Trajectory: the "over" and "cuts" variants broke; the "gradual cadence" variant is under heavy pressure.** Core CPI ran at 3.7% annualised, Waller and Logan openly discussed a faster pace, and the 2Y jumped +18bp (WSJ, Thu 11/12). The 10Y reached 5.10% and gilts 5.30% (FT, Fri 11/13). One caution: Friday's FT framed rate cuts as the prevailing prior, but the WSJ had already reported a September hike and 78% odds of two more (WSJ, Tue 11/10). The cuts variant was stale before Friday's edition called it pressured.

**Prior:** AI infrastructure is politically untouchable (WSJ, Mon 11/9) / power and siting are secondary, solvable constraints (FT, Mon 11/9).
**Trajectory: confirmed further under pressure.**
- Turbines appeared on both sides of the ledger: Anthropic's $3.4bn OnePWR order (FT, Mon 11/9) against SoftBank's turbine-related writedown (WSJ, Wed 11/11).

**Counter-prior:** A Democratic Congress is unambiguously bearish for the AI and mega-cap trade (WSJ, Tue 11/10).

**Trajectory: confirmed under pressure across three editions.**

Thursday, both papers challenged the opposite claim — "AI capex has durably resumed" (WSJ, Thu 11/12; FT, Thu 11/12). Friday challenged "Nvidia is reputational only, buy the dip" (FT, Fri 11/13). The narrative oscillated with the equity tape while credit data moved in one direction only (Arc 1). Treat equity-led consensus calls as noise this week and credit-led ones as signal.

**Consensus:** The blue wave is a governing mandate (WSJ, Mon 11/9).
**Trajectory: challenge supported.** The Tuesday gridlock reporting and Friday's GOP re-narration around AI backlash point the same way: a protest vote with diffuse causes, not a programme (WSJ, Tue 11/10; FT, Fri 11/13).

## 10. Steel-Man of the Week

**Consensus view:** The AI buildout's financing structure is cracking — circular prepayments, a distressed CoreWeave curve, banks offloading risk, IG concentration caps, and Nvidia's dealmaker gone — and the equity rebound is short-covering ahead of a credit event.

**Steel-man:**
- **Spreads did not move system-wide.** The only index-level spread figure of the week was HY at 282bp, mid-range, despite a 32% VIX spike (WSJ, Mon 11/9). Every later "credit stress" data point was single-issuer (CoreWeave, SoftBank) or about intermediaries managing limits (banks, IG funds).
- **The two most bearish credit data points carry their own caveats.** Lex said CoreWeave's bonds suggest "debt investors might be too bearish" (FT, Thu 11/12). FT's Friday concentration piece says any widening would be portfolio-construction "indigestion" independent of issuer quality (FT, Fri 11/13). A technical squeeze is something to trade, not a solvency signal.
- **Demand rose through the stress.** CoreWeave beat Q3 and is drawing ~$9bn of customer prepayments in three months — customers paying cash in advance is evidence of demand, not of circular financing (WSJ, Thu 11/12). TSMC's October revenue rose 60% year-on-year and it approved $40.93bn of 2027 capex (WSJ, Thu 11/12), Siemens Energy raised its FY2030 targets the same day, and Goldman raised its forecast to $8.5tn the day after Huang resigned (FT, Fri 11/13).
- **The bearish IPO read was contradicted.** Wednesday's "Anthropic IPO repricing lower" (WSJ, Wed 11/11, the thinnest brief) was contradicted 24 hours later by heavy demand pushing the target toward $1.5tn (FT, Thu 11/12) and by Hyperliquid implying $1.8tn (WSJ, Thu 11/12).
- **SoftBank's stress is a collateral-price problem.** Its LTV rose because Arm fell ~60%, not because operating cash flows failed (FT, Tue 11/10).
- **Valuations reset rather than inflated.** The S&P's forward P/E fell to 17.7x–18.27x against a 19.56x ten-year average (WSJ, Tue 11/10; WSJ, Thu 11/12).
- **The case:** Read across the week, the evidence describes a capital-intensive buildout repricing its cost of capital in its riskiest tranches while end-demand keeps accelerating — a funding-mix problem the strongest balance sheets (TSMC, the hyperscalers) are already filling. It does not describe a credit event in waiting.
