## 1. The Week in One Paragraph

On Monday morning the AI selloff could still be read as a Nvidia governance shock with a single fix: get Jensen Huang back. By Thursday that reading had been tested and failed. Huang was reinstated, Monday's session produced the largest one-day US market-cap gain on record (S&P +4.73%, Nasdaq +6.51%, SOX +14.03%; WSJ, Tue 11/24), and within two sessions the Nasdaq 100 was down 4.2% on the day and on course for a near-10% monthly drop, "one of its deepest slumps since the 2008 global financial crisis" (FT, Thu 11/26). Through all of it the credit market never joined the equity rally: Nvidia's five-year CDS stayed ~53% wider than early October and its active bonds fell on the rally day (WSJ, Tue 11/24; WSJ, Wed 11/25), CoreWeave's 9.75% 2031s recovered only to 80.25 (WSJ, Tue 11/24), Anthropic broke its $83 IPO price (WSJ, Wed 11/25; FT, Wed 11/25; FT, Thu 11/26), and Nscale flagged a 2027 financing need after Microsoft walked from a £3.2bn contract (FT, Thu 11/26). Fixing governance did not fix financing. The open question is now credit, and what drives it: whether the AI financing stack (vendor finance, neocloud debt, IPO float, insurer-held private paper) can refinance at the rates the market is now charging. Personnel at Nvidia no longer decides it.

## 2. Theme Arcs

### Arc 1 — The Nvidia/Huang crisis moved from a personnel question to a multi-front legal and political liability, and reinstatement made it worse

- **Start (Mon):** Huang out; interim CEO Colette Kress defending $390B of purchase commitments after reporting that ~30 years of Nvidia–TSMC dealmaking ran without written contracts (WSJ, Mon 11/23). Bessent softening his call for Huang's ouster under Silicon Valley pressure even as Commerce's Megaspeed criminal probe continued; Trump dismissing the MSS honeytrap framing (FT, Mon 11/23).
- **Tue:** Board reinstates Huang unanimously, citing "input from senior levels of the United States government"; Trump: "I convinced them... I made the deal" (WSJ, Tue 11/24). FT's Big Read: no risk committee, no succession plan, no board member with government/defence/intelligence background (FT, Tue 11/24). WSJ links it to the Intel precedent (9.9% government stake as the price of Lip-Bu Tan keeping his job). Four probes stay open: SEC accounting, DOJ/Commerce Megaspeed, California AG subpoena, congressional hearings Dec. 1–3 (WSJ, Tue 11/24).
- **Wed:** Reporting shows Trump, Vance, Bessent and AG Blanche personally worked the board over 48 hours (WSJ, Wed 11/25). Commerce adds Megaspeed and Alibaba's Apex to the Entity List the same Monday as the reinstatement. Commerce had reportedly blocked tougher Megaspeed action for two years "during trade talks" (WSJ, Wed 11/25). Bernstein calls the board process "clown-like"; FBI and SEC probes cited (FT, Wed 11/25).
- **Thu:** Liability list widens: three congressional hearings next week (closed session Tue, Senate Banking open session Thu Dec. 3), SEC, DOJ/Commerce, California AG, a new Missouri AG inquiry into state-pension harm, and two shareholder suits alleging the board concealed the Alice Huang relationship and alleging insider trading. FT also reports Nvidia stopped disclosing the scale of its Singapore revenue shortly after booking nearly half of global sales there (FT, Thu 11/26).
- **Status: ESCALATING.** Each edition added at least one new investigative or legal front, and no edition retired one. The Dec. 1–3 hearings are the first hard catalyst.

## 6. Geopolitics & Policy Arc

**Dominant thread: the US–China chip export-control corridor.** It was tagged as the hotspot in three of seven editions and appears as a sub-thread in all seven, and it is structural. It started Monday as an enforcement-leakage story: the Megaspeed/Novagate network moved at least $300M of Nvidia hardware in five months while Megaspeed held official cloud-partner status (WSJ, Mon 11/23), and Bessent and Trump sent opposite signals on Huang (FT, Mon 11/23). The calculus changed Tuesday, when the President traded regulatory assurances for Huang's reinstatement and deferred the institutional check to Congress, the SEC and the DOJ (WSJ, Tue 11/24). FT also traced the Megaspeed→Inspur→Aivres OEM-loophole structure (FT, Tue 11/24). On Wednesday operational enforcement moved the other way: Megaspeed, Apex and their Thai and Malaysian affiliates went on the Entity List, and Singapore's PM said the city-state would not be "used and abused as a conduit" (FT, Wed 11/25). The listing narrows the "neo-cloud" channel that Gregory Allen puts at 51% of Nvidia sales (FT, Wed 11/25). Huawei's conviction on 10 of 12 counts landed the same days (WSJ, Wed 11/25; FT, Thu 11/26). On Thursday the corridor merged with trade policy: China's tariff can double to 40% on rare-earth non-compliance, and the truce runs only to Jan. 10 (FT, Thu 11/26). Nvidia's halted disclosure of its Singapore revenue (FT, Thu 11/26) ties the corridor directly to Nvidia's own reporting. **Assessment: structural.** The enforcement apparatus is tightening at the operating level and loosening at the political level at the same time, and the gap between the two is what the Dec. 1–3 hearings and an incoming Democratic House (January) will be pricing.

**Parallel hotspot with a different trajectory: the Russia–Europe hybrid and strategic escalation corridor.** Its trajectory was steadily upward, and it escalated in every FT edition. The beats: Russian missile/drone incursion into Poland during a NATO exercise (WSJ, Mon 11/23) and Kaliningrad sirens (FT, Mon 11/23); Germany warning Russia may launch a nuclear-armed "Sputnik-S" satellite within 60 days (FT, Tue 11/24); Patriot interceptors stalled at the Pentagon and a €70bn 2027 Ukraine funding gap (FT, Wed 11/25); Merz in the Bundestag naming Baltic, Poland, Black Sea and space escalation, IRGC-linked sabotage of French supermarkets with possible Russian collaboration, a plot against Rheinmetall's CEO, and the US dropping commercial inducements for "land for peace" (FT, Thu 11/26). ## 10. Steel-Man of the Week

**Consensus view:** The AI trade's financing stack is cracking: credit markets, not the equity rally, are telling the truth, and the week's stress is the start of a multi-channel de-rating.

**Steel-man:** The credit evidence is thinner than the narrative built on it. Nvidia's "widening" was ~0.03pp intraday on its active bonds and a CDS move measured from a very low base (WSJ, Tue 11/24); the Wednesday-edition version is that bonds "declined slightly" (WSJ, Wed 11/25). That is caution, not distress, at a company whose forward P/E had already compressed to 8.2x (WSJ, Tue 11/24). The visible distress sits in a narrow tier: neoclouds (CoreWeave, Nebius, Nscale) and one IPO whose weakness has a mechanical supply explanation. Anthropic floated with no lockup, ~300M employee shares sold immediately and another ~300M unlock Dec. 15 (WSJ, Mon 11/23). Analysts said the deal "raised too much money" (WSJ, Wed 11/25). Private-market demand for AI equity held up the same week: Databricks is in talks at ~$225B, up ~20% (WSJ, Mon 11/23), and Stripe's tender sits above $200B (WSJ, Wed 11/25). TSMC rose 9.9% and the Kospi 6.3% in Tuesday's session (FT, Wed 11/25). The macro backdrop was not recessionary either: 3.5% annualised Q3 GDP (FT, Mon 11/23), retail guidance raised across TJX, Walmart, Dollar Tree, Abercrombie and Best Buy (FT, Mon 11/23; WSJ, Wed 11/25), and a 10Y that *fell* ~8–9bp, easing financial conditions (FT, Thu 11/26). Even the week's most-cited fragility sources disagree with each other. UBS credits AI with "more than half" of GDP growth (WSJ, Mon 11/23) while Barclays says 25% (WSJ, Wed 11/25). FT's Thursday tariff analysis calls the new duties "largely codifying" even as the same edition blames tariff headlines for the volatility (FT, Thu 11/26). On this reading, the week's damage is a deleveraging and float-supply event concentrated in the weakest borrowers, which is what gold's risk-correlated selling also suggests (FT, Thu 11/26).

## 13. Research Call Questions

1. **Thesis test (§3).** The "selloff is contained" consensus retreated daily: from "resolved" (Tue) to "vindicated" (Wed) to "a positioning shock, not fundamental" (FT, Thu 11/26). Nvidia's bonds never rallied (WSJ, Tue 11/24; WSJ, Wed 11/25), while the 10Y fell ~8–9bp on the week (FT, Thu 11/26). **Is the credit market pricing Nvidia-specific legal risk or AI-ecosystem refinancing risk, and which of the two would a clean Dec. 3 hearing actually remove?**

2. **Counterparty/credit.** Two independent threads point at insurer balance sheets: $24B of Walter-affiliated related-party loans across Clear Spring, Delaware Life and EquiTrust (WSJ, Mon 11/23; WSJ, Tue 11/24), and $512bn of Egan-Jones private letter ratings running ~3 notches generous (FT, Wed 11/25). **What is our look-through exposure to insurer-held private paper rated by a single small agency, and what forced-selling cascade follows if NAIC or the states move to haircut private letter ratings?**

3. **UNTESTED WWHTBT (§4).** The assumption that no Nvidia investigation yields adverse findings (WSJ, Tue 11/24) went untested, but the surface widened from four probes to congressional, SEC, DOJ/Commerce, FBI, two state AGs and two shareholder suits (FT, Wed 11/25; FT, Thu 11/26). **Which of these has the shortest path to a disclosed finding, and how do we hedge desk exposure to Nvidia-linked neocloud credit (CoreWeave 2031s at 80.25) against that specific catalyst?**

Wolf argues the TPI may be unusable for a nationalist government (FT, Wed 11/25). **At what OAT-Bund level does the market begin to price TPI non-availability, and is that threshold visible yet in French bank CDS or in OAT-BTP relative value?**

5. **Cross-asset regime.** Gold moved with risk on every observation this week: down with equities (FT, Mon 11/23), up in the melt-up (WSJ, Tue 11/24; FT, Tue 11/24), down in the rout to a 14-month low (FT, Thu 11/26). The 10Y simultaneously took the haven bid. **Has the desk's tail-hedge book been relying on a gold–equity correlation that no longer holds, and should duration replace gold as the primary risk-off hedge into the Jan. 10 tariff-truce expiry?**
