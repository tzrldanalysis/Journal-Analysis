## 1. The Week in One Paragraph

On Monday the open question was whether Jensen Huang's ouster was an idiosyncratic governance failure or a crack in the AI trade (WSJ, Mon 11/16; FT, Mon 11/16). By Friday that question had been replaced by a harder one: can the AI build-out be financed now that its de facto lender of last resort has stepped back? Nvidia's $500bn Wall Street chip-financing consortium has deployed zero dollars, interim CEO Kress is "rigorously evaluating" every guarantee, and Nvidia caps residual-value guarantees at 25% against Broadcom's 75–85% (WSJ, Fri 11/20). In the same four days banks started selling data-centre loans at 85–90 cents (FT, Wed 11/18), the ECB put AI-linked off-balance-sheet commitments "well in excess" of $4tn with explicit dotcom/subprime parallels (FT, Thu 11/19), and AI-exposed software credit cleared only at 12–13% yields (FT, Thu 11/19). Record equity issuance did not plug the gap. Anthropic's $81bn IPO priced above range, and its debut day was the S&P's sixth straight loss, with all 11 sectors red, tech (XLK) in a formal bear market and the VIX at 28.44 (WSJ, Fri 11/20). The Fed's glide-path assumption also broke: a December hike is now "all but certain" (WSJ, Thu 11/19). The 10-year still fell four sessions running. That is a deleveraging signature, not a macro rotation.

## 2. Theme Arcs

### Arc 1 — AI financing stress moved down the capital structure, from equity to lenders to supervisors to the guarantor itself, and is escalating

- **Mon:** The stress was framed as a vendor-balance-sheet problem. SemiAnalysis put Nvidia's off-balance-sheet credit guarantees at $530bn, up from $184bn, with a GE Capital comparison (WSJ, Mon 11/16). FT framed it as "credit anticipates equity": data-centre-linked equities have lost $4.2tn since June, with Deutsche Bank's Ryan saying the market is "finally benchmarking to what credit was already telling you" (FT, Mon 11/16).
- **Tue:** It became a named credit event. CoreWeave 2031s had traded >10pts over Treasuries ("distressed"), and UBS estimated a Nvidia backstop pullback could remove $50bn+ (WSJ, Tue 11/17). ECB VP Vujcic previewed "circular financial arrangements" as a stability risk (WSJ, Tue 11/17). FT added a credit-insurance tenor mismatch (cover under 1 year against much longer loans) and record $35bn European SRT issuance with EBA "circles of risk" warnings (FT, Tue 11/17). **[WEAK SIGNAL upgraded]** WSJ Tuesday's weak signal, "circular/opaque vendor financing surfacing across unrelated stories", became WSJ's *dominant* theme on Thu ("AI capex supercycle financed by circular, concentrated vendor credit") and again on Fri ("AI capital formation is outrunning AI credit discipline"). It is the week's clearest case of the daily process catching a theme two days before it became the lead.
- **Wed:** Lenders started acting. ING and SocGen put up to $800mn of Oracle-linked data-centre debt up for sale at ~90c, with Project Jupiter in the mid-80s after a force-majeure power notice. BoE and ECB opened formal supervisory reviews, and a $63bn Anthropic debt package and a $40bn SpaceX financing are headed to the 144A market (FT, Wed 11/18). WSJ put Nvidia forward commitments at ~$390bn, up from $279bn (WSJ, Wed 11/18).
- **Thu:** Supervisors and spreads confirmed it. The ECB FSR (>$4tn, "abrupt repricing" warning) landed, and AI credit bifurcated: Qualtrics needed an 8% coupon at ~80c (12–13% yield) while Proofpoint drew $7bn of orders at ~8.75% (FT, Thu 11/19). Kress said guarantees would continue only "selectively" (WSJ, Thu 11/19).
- **Fri:** The guarantor retreated. The consortium has deployed $0, the 25% RVG cap stands, Nvidia's eight listed portfolio stakes have shed $820bn since Oct 6, and CoreWeave fell 6.1% to $44.91 on "circular financing" fears (WSJ, Fri 11/20). Contagion widened to AMD (Hunterbrook alleges circular financing via data-centre lease backstops, shares about 9% lower) and to Meta (a $60bn non-voting share sale frozen by Delaware litigation) (FT, Fri 11/20).
- **Status: ESCALATING.** Each day added a new layer of the capital structure. Nothing in Friday's editions eases it.

## 3. Thesis Tracker

**Two priors broke outright inside the week. Both are about financing rather than demand.**

**Prior/Consensus:** The Fed is on a path back to cuts as the economy softens (WSJ, Wed 11/18), restated as "the October pause signals a glide path toward 2027 cuts" (WSJ, Thu 11/19).
**Trajectory:** **Broke outright.** Wednesday's data was soft: retail sales +0.2%, industrial production −0.4%, NAHB at a 14-year low. Even so, markets priced 98% odds of zero cuts and hikes by March (WSJ, Wed 11/18). On Thursday the minutes showed nearly every official expects further tightening, Waller floated 50bp, and a December hike is "all but certain" (WSJ, Thu 11/19). FT had flagged the hawkish setup on Monday: dissents for *slower* hikes and TD expecting a hike at each of the next three meetings (FT, Mon 11/16).

**Prior/Consensus:** The AI capex boom is creditworthy and insulated from politics and financing fragility (WSJ, Tue 11/17), restated as "AI capex is effectively self-funding and low-credit-risk because the balance sheets behind it are too large to let financing gaps matter" (WSJ, Fri 11/20). Related consensus challenges: "self-funding and inevitable" (FT, Wed 11/18) and "self-funding and durable" (WSJ, Thu 11/19).
**Trajectory:** **Broke for the guarantor leg; still under pressure for the lender leg.** The "too big to let gaps matter" balance sheets are the ones that pulled back. The consortium of Apollo, Blackstone, BlackRock, Brookfield, Goldman and KKR has deployed $0, Nvidia's RVG cap is 25%, and Kress is evaluating every guarantee (WSJ, Fri 11/20). European lenders are selling the loans (FT, Wed 11/18). The defence offered by later reporting is real but narrow: Proofpoint's oversubscribed deal shows credit is discriminating by issuer, not closing outright (FT, Thu 11/19).

**Prior/Consensus — the week's consensus flipped mid-week, and the dailies' contrarian stance flipped with it.** On Mon–Tue three of four editions steel-manned *against the bear case*:

- FT Mon challenged "AI capex is entering a structural bust".
- WSJ Tue challenged "the AI capex supercycle is broadly cracking".
- FT Tue challenged "the selloff signals the bubble bursting".

Only WSJ Mon attacked the bull case ("Huang is idiosyncratic"). From Wednesday all six editions attacked the *bull* case, once Nvidia's beat and Anthropic's above-range pricing re-established it (WSJ Wed, FT Wed, WSJ Thu, FT Thu, WSJ Fri, FT Fri).
**Trajectory:** The Mon–Tue bull steel-mans rested on three things: the equal-weight S&P outperforming (WSJ, Tue 11/17; FT, Mon 11/16), bankers "considering raising" Anthropic's price (FT, Tue 11/17), and hyperscaler FCF turning in 2028 (FT, Tue 11/17). Anthropic's pricing then materialised and did not re-anchor the tape (WSJ, Fri 11/20). So the bull case was **confirmed on capital-raising capacity and undermined on market transmission.**

**Prior/Consensus:** Concentrated "long America" positioning is a safe default (FT, Mon 11/16), restated as "US equity exceptionalism as a durable secular overweight" (FT, Thu 11/19).
**Trajectory:** **Further under pressure in commentary, not confirmed by the tape.** Goldman's Oppenheimer said "the era where equities were entirely dominated by the US is over", and Morgan Stanley cited a "structural break" (FT, Thu 11/19). But Europe did not decouple. European indices fell for a fourth straight session on Friday (FT, Fri 11/20), and ASML −5.3% / Stoxx tech −3.9% tracked Nasdaq on Wednesday (FT, Wed 11/18). What the week shows is a US-tech drawdown, not a rotation into Europe.

**Prior/Consensus:** Trump-negotiated sanctions deals lower consumer costs (WSJ, Mon 11/16). The Russian diesel waiver produced $6.25 vs $6.20, flat.
**Trajectory:** **Partly defended on price, not on mechanism.** Energy Secretary Wright says diesel fell to $5.93 from a $6.53 record. But no Russian diesel cargo has landed in the US, and the decline is attributed to returning Gulf flows and sanctions-relief *expectations* (WSJ, Fri 11/20).

**Prior/Consensus:** Dollar primacy maps onto allied dependence on US leadership (FT, Wed 11/18).
**Trajectory:** **Under pressure, partly offset.** Canada–China tariff relief (WSJ, Wed 11/18; FT, Wed 11/18), the EU's foreign-subsidies tightening (FT, Thu 11/19) and EU-China managed trade (FT, Fri 11/20) extend the hedging pattern. The Denmark–Greenland–US deal is a counter-example of an ally settling with Washington on its terms (WSJ, Fri 11/20).

Single-day priors not tracked further: low unemployment as a sign of labour-market health (FT, Tue 11/17), and US bank deregulation being unambiguously bullish (FT, Fri 11/20).

## 10. Steel-Man of the Week

**Consensus view:** The AI build-out's circular, vendor-guaranteed financing is now cracking into a systemic credit problem, and the week's selloff is the market pricing that.

**Steel-man:**
- The week's hardest data contradicts systemic stress. Treasuries rallied four straight sessions (WSJ, Fri 11/20). Investment-grade spreads started the week at 32bp and high-yield at 264bp (WSJ, Mon 11/16). The only quantified IG widening was Toyota Motor Credit +52bp and JPMorgan +10bp (WSJ, Wed 11/18). A credit crisis does not look like that.
- Credit is discriminating, not closing. Proofpoint drew $7bn of orders for a $2bn deal the same day Qualtrics struggled (FT, Thu 11/19). The bank loan sales total $800mn (FT, Wed 11/18), small against the >$4tn the ECB cites (FT, Thu 11/19).
- The "retreat" of the guarantor can be read as risk *reduction*. A 25% RVG cap against Broadcom's 75–85% (WSJ, Fri 11/20) means Nvidia is declining to become the GE Capital that Monday's op-ed feared (WSJ, Mon 11/16).
- Demand kept beating. Revenue was $115.8bn against $108.9bn (WSJ, Wed 11/18), backlog rose to $2.5tn from $2tn in three months, and guidance was raised to 75% (WSJ, Thu 11/19). Anthropic raised $81bn above range, with BlackRock filled at only half its order (WSJ, Fri 11/20).
- The bear case also rests on an unstable number. Nvidia's "exposure" was $530bn on Monday (WSJ, Mon 11/16), $390bn on Wednesday (WSJ, Wed 11/18) and $518bn on Friday (WSJ, Fri 11/20) under three different definitions. A thesis whose headline figure changes definition daily is weaker than its rhetoric.
- The more defensible read is a crowded mega-cap equity unwind meeting a hawkish Fed. The equal-weight S&P was up 15.3% YTD against 8.3% for the cap-weighted index on Tuesday (WSJ, Tue 11/17). The credit system is showing price discovery, not contagion.
