## 2. Finance & Markets

**Rates.** The dominant cross-asset event is the divergence in central bank communication, not policy itself. Warsh held the Fed's rate but let the market do the tightening for him — 30-year yields at 5.24 per cent (highest since 2007) even as two-year yields fell to 4.24 per cent on cut repricing. That's a steepener driven by inflation-risk premium, not growth optimism, which is the worst kind for term-funding costs on corporate and household debt. Contrast: the Bank of England held rates and 10-year gilts *fell* 5bp to 4.99 per cent — a clean, unsurprised market reaction — while Bunds rose just 1bp to 3.17 per cent. The Fed is now the outlier in market legibility among G7 central banks, and traders are pricing a two-thirds probability of a quarter-point hike next meeting, which is itself an indictment: markets are pricing the Fed's next move for it.

**Credit.** Moody's flagged that hyperscalers are becoming "asset-heavy" businesses whose "relationship with capital, risk and credit [are] being redefined" — Alphabet's long-term debt is approaching $100bn (from $11bn a year ago) alongside a fresh $85bn equity raise and first-ever cash burn. This is a credit-desk-relevant shift: names historically underwritten as cash-rich search/software franchises are migrating toward capital-intensive, debt-funded infrastructure profiles typical of utilities or telecoms, without utility-grade regulated returns. Watch spread behaviour on hyperscaler paper into year-end.

## 6. Prior Under Pressure

**Prior:** Central bank gold buying provides a durable, informal price floor under the bullion market, underpinning the multi-year gold rally as part of a broader de-dollarisation trend.

**Pressure:** The World Gold Council's data revision slashed estimated Q1 central bank purchases from 244 tonnes to just 57 tonnes — the lowest first-quarter level in over 15 years — while H1 official-sector buying (345 tonnes) is the weakest six-month total since 2022. Central banks still account for close to a third of Q2 demand, per the WGC's John Reade, but disclosure quality has declined since 2022 sanctions on Russia pushed diversifying states toward opacity, and some official institutions (Turkey, Russia, Azerbaijan) were net sellers in the period. Gold is already down almost 30 per cent from its January peak; a mechanism the market has treated as structural and one-directional is showing early signs of being neither.
