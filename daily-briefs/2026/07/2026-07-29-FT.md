--- 6. PRIOR UNDER PRESSURE ---

Prior: Central banks, and the incoming Fed chair specifically, can treat AI-driven capex and productivity gains as a straightforwardly disinflationary supply-side story that justifies looser policy without inflation risk.

Pressure: [FT ANGLE] Two separate central banking institutions challenged this the same day, on the record. --- 7. WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE ---

Implication: Europe's wind-turbine makers successfully consolidate into a scale competitor to Chinese manufacturers without falling foul of EU competition law.
Assumption: The EU's new merger guidelines must be applied liberally enough in practice to clear a deal that would otherwise be presumptively too concentrated — Kepler Cheuvreux's William Mackie explicitly says a Vestas-Nordex tie-up "might be too concentrated to pass merger authorities" under a conventional reading.
Fragility: Vestas's own CEO conditions his support on "some exempt rule or new legislation" beyond the guidelines as currently written; if regulators apply the old competition framework in practice despite the new policy language, the entire consolidation logic collapses and Europe's wind sector remains fragmented against a Chinese competitor that installed more than double Vestas's global capacity in a single year.

Implication: Hyperscalers keep financing AI capex at serviceable cost despite record CDS spreads, so the current debt-funded buildout — Meta's Texas data centre, Nvidia's $50bn Hut 8 lease, the prospective $250bn Nvidia backstop for OpenAI's Ohio facility — avoids a genuine funding crunch.
Assumption: Underlying AI demand growth (Singapore's concrete >70% AI-driven export growth is the clearest evidence cited today) continues to outrun financing costs long enough for revenue to catch up to spending before credit markets close the window.
Fragility: The financing structure is already relying on increasingly circular arrangements — Nvidia backstopping OpenAI's lease of a SoftBank-built facility so it can in turn sublease to neocloud operators like CoreWeave, a structure the piece explicitly calls "circular financing." Any single failure point in that chain — OpenAI's own funding, SoftBank's balance sheet, or a Nvidia demand air-pocket — could freeze the entire structure at once, and CDS markets are already pricing exactly that circularity risk today, not a distant one.
