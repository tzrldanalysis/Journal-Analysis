## 3. Geopolitics & Policy

## 12. One To Watch

The hedge-fund basis trade's growing share of the Treasury market — now a record 7%, more than double five years ago — deserves more attention than the brief, process-focused treatment it got today. It sits at the intersection of nearly every other theme in this edition: it's a direct consequence of pension funds and foreign sovereign wealth funds retreating from Treasurys (which is itself a consequence of a decade of low rates reshaping return targets), it's happening exactly as the Fed prepares a hawkish pivot that could shock rate expectations, and the New York Fed's active outreach to multimanager platforms shows regulators already see it as a genuine systemic vulnerability rather than a market curiosity. If the Fed's Wednesday move — or a subsequent oil/geopolitical shock — triggers a rapid, correlated unwind of these leveraged positions, it could produce exactly the kind of Treasury-market dysfunction that would force the Fed's hand regardless of its inflation mandate. This is a slow-building structural risk hiding in plain sight underneath the day's more visible Fed and oil stories.
