## 1. Today's Themes

Washington is simultaneously threatening to weaponize dollar-system access against an ally's financial sector (Israel) and watching another ally (Saudi Arabia) choose to stay inside that same system rather than risk it.

## 3. Geopolitics & Policy

**Israel settlement sanctions.** Mechanism: the UK, alongside France, Canada, Ireland, Spain, Belgium and Norway, is moving on goods bans (wine, fruit, olive oil) covering roughly £11mn of £12bn total UK-Israel trade — a fraction. The far more consequential measure under consideration is sanctions on banks and insurers servicing settlements; UK officials are taking six to nine months to finalize the legislation. Market implication: London is one of the world's top three financial centres, and extending sanctions to bank connectivity "could carry serious results" for Israeli financial-sector access (Strum). Geopolitical signal: this is escalation by degree — more than 100 new settlements approved under Netanyahu (nearly doubling the pre-term total of 127) — with Whitehall explicitly holding "wriggle room" pending Israel's own elections next month. **[FT ANGLE]** London is treating this as a City-of-London financial-connectivity question first and a trade-policy question second — a structurally different framing than a US paper is likely to offer.

**Saudi Arabia's mBridge withdrawal.** Mechanism: Riyadh formally exited the China-led cross-border CBDC platform after "successfully completing" its proof-of-concept phase, joining the Bank for International Settlements in stepping back (BIS left in October 2024 under reported US pressure). Trump has threatened 100% tariffs on Brics countries pursuing dollar alternatives. Market implication: reduces near-term de-dollarization momentum optically, though China, Hong Kong, Thailand, the UAE and now Macau continue rolling the platform out commercially. Geopolitical signal: even a longstanding US ally with real Gulf-Washington friction elsewhere (oil prices, Houthi response, Israel-adjacent sanctions spillover) is choosing to stay inside US financial leverage rather than test it.

**Iran's overland trade pivot.** Structural/institutional: more than 80% of Iran's pre-war trade moved by sea; the US naval blockade and Hormuz disruption have forced a shift to land routes through Turkey, Iraq, the Caspian and rail links via Turkmenistan/Kazakhstan. Second-order effects: Majid Reza Hariri (Iran-China Chamber of Commerce) estimates moving China trade overland instead of by sea costs an extra $18bn a year; Iraq's temporary closure of two border crossings after the Saudi pipeline drone attacks shows how entangled Iran's evasion routes now are with the broader regional energy war.

## 5. Steel-Man

**Consensus view:** Frontier AI labs (Anthropic, OpenAI) deserve trillion-dollar-plus valuations because usage and revenue growth — 250x token growth since last year, Anthropic's annualised revenue soaring past $65bn — is outrunning even bulls' expectations and justifies treating them as generational compounders.

**Steel-man:** Lex's own reporting undercuts the compounding-moat thesis at every turn. OpenAI has already overtaken Anthropic in weekly spend for the first time in two and a half years, meaning Anthropic's vaunted retention edge (22.5% of users still active after 12 months versus OpenAI's 15.2%) is a thin moat against genuine competitive churn. Ramp's Eric Glyman cut AI spend 40% purely by routing tasks across models — direct evidence enterprise customers already treat frontier models as substitutable, not sticky, which is precisely the "commodity cognition" dynamic Palantir's Alex Karp describes, where value accrues to whoever owns the data or application layer, not the model-maker. The "SaaSpocalypse" narrative cuts both ways: if AI genuinely disrupts a $500bn slice of software value that fast, it's also proof that models are becoming cheap, fungible infrastructure — the same commoditization dynamic that caps the labs' own pricing power over time. And Nvidia's own hedge — acquiring Hugging Face, the leading open-weight distribution platform, even as it invests in both OpenAI and Anthropic — is a tell that the industry's biggest AI winner is positioning against, not for, durable closed-model pricing power.

## 6. Prior Under Pressure

**Prior:** AI lab valuations deserve to be judged on a different scale than normal software companies because usage growth this explosive (250x tokens in a year) has no real precedent and justifies suspending ordinary multiple-compression skepticism.

**Pressure:** The entire multi-trillion-dollar bull case for Anthropic and OpenAI implicitly assumes the labs themselves capture the economic value AI creates — an assumption nearly every major AI-adjacent player is actively positioning against. Nvidia hedges via Hugging Face. Salesforce's Marc Benioff simultaneously calls the "SaaSpocalypse" "crazy nonsense" while striking a "Claudeforce" tie-up that blurs exactly the line he's dismissing. Karp's "commodity cognition" framing explicitly argues value migrates to the application and data layer, not the model. And OpenAI's own disclosed economics — expenses "far outrunning" even a 10x revenue increase through 2030, requiring a $1.2tn valuation just to keep funding a $280bn burn — show the supposed frontrunner's unit economics getting *worse*, not better, the faster it scales. If the leading lab's growth is inversely correlated with capital efficiency, the "growth justifies the premium" prior is much harder to hold with confidence.
