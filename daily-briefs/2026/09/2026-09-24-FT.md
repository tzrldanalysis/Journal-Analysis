## 3. Geopolitics & Policy

**Sanctions mechanics — Rosneft/A7.** The mechanism: Rosneft-linked oil traders (via the "Coral network," now trading as 2Rivers/Coral Energy under sanctioned Azerbaijani businessmen Etibar Eyyub and Tahir Garayev) deposit UAE-dirham export earnings into front-company accounts in Dubai; A7 then matches each dirham transfer with a rouble payment inside Russia, letting Rosneft access hard currency abroad while evading Swift. At least $22mn in dirhams flowed from Sibur (Russia's largest petrochemicals group) alone; Evraz (still majority-owned by sanctioned oligarch Roman Abramovich pre-2022 restructuring) is also implicated. Market implication: any Western bank with UAE correspondent exposure faces latent compliance risk; geopolitical signal: sanctions enforcement four years in is still leaking at meaningful scale, which should discount confidence in the newer Iran campaign (below) achieving faster results. **US-Iran mechanics:** Bessent's threat to "shut down all the Iranian airlines... around the world" has already prompted Oman, Azerbaijan and Iraq to suspend flights and Georgia to suspend flights to Tbilisi, part of what Washington calls an "economic D-Day" campaign; Turkey's compliance record is historically mixed and will be the real test given ~80,000 Iranian residence-permit holders there.

**Institutional/structural dynamics — Saudi-US.** [FT ANGLE] Trump's refusal to join direct strikes on the Houthis, and his earlier veto of a joint Hormuz-protection operation over Saudi basing objections, is read in Washington as demonstrating the US "will not necessarily fight when Saudi Arabia believes its vital interests are threatened" (ret.
