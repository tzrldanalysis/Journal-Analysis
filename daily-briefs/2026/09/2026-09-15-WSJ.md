## 1. Today's Themes

**Warsh is being walked into a hike he cannot politically afford to look comfortable making.** The 10-year Treasury crossed 5% intraday Monday for the first time since 2007, closing at 4.96%, and investors now price roughly 90% odds of a Fed rate increase Wednesday — the opposite of what Trump has spent a year demanding. Trump's public line that he "100% respects the independence of Kevin Warsh" reads as calculated distance-taking ahead of a decision the White House won't like.

**AI's own architects are triggering the selloff they warned about.** Dario Amodei, Sam Altman, Demis Hassabis and Elon Musk jointly called for a development slowdown Saturday; Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon quit over extinction-risk fears the same week a colleague put the odds of AI killing everyone within a decade above 10%. Markets responded immediately and unevenly: the PHLX Semiconductor Index fell 5.9% (worst day since July) while cybersecurity names — CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks, Fortinet — rallied double digits on a rotation out of AI-compute exposure and into "safety" plays. This is a market repricing a risk that used to be rhetorical.

**The Iran war is metastasizing into a second maritime chokepoint crisis.** Houthi forces seized Bab al-Mandeb and Perim Island over the weekend, layering a new Red Sea threat on top of the existing Strait of Hormuz squeeze. Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline, the kingdom's Hormuz bypass, remains crippled from a September 10 drone attack; Yanbu loadings collapsed from over 4 million barrels a day in the spring to 1.1 million in August, and total Saudi output has fallen to 6 million barrels a day — the lowest in more than three decades. Brent jumped more than 4% Monday to above $109; WTI settled at $101.39.

**The Gulf's AI data-center bet is colliding with the war it was supposed to be insulated from.** Six months after Iranian drone strikes on Amazon Web Services facilities in Abu Dhabi and Bahrain, roughly 130 and 140 computing services respectively remain offline. OpenAI still hasn't signed its lease for the planned 5-gigawatt UAE complex, and Emirati officials are now discussing burying future data centers underground. The infrastructure risk to the AI buildout looks more physical and more immediate than the regulatory risk getting all the attention.

**Counterparty stress is building inside the private-credit-into-insurance pipeline.** Regulators are probing $20 billion in investments by Mark Walter's insurance conglomerate (Delaware Life, Clear Spring) with affiliated entities that weren't properly disclosed, echoing a pattern already flagged around Apollo- and KKR-backed insurers. ## 6. Prior Under Pressure

**Prior:** A Warsh-led Fed would prove genuinely independent of White House pressure and would be inclined to move slowly, giving the economy room to absorb the Iran-war energy shock without a rate increase heading into the midterms.

**Pressure:** That prior is being tested in real time and is losing ground. Investors now price roughly 90% odds of a hike Wednesday — the opposite of what the president has demanded for a year. The genuine risk to the independence thesis isn't that Warsh caves to Trump — it's that raising rates precisely when investors widely expect it, right before an election, will look indistinguishable from capitulating to inflation data that arrived on the White House's own tariff-and-energy watch, regardless of which way Warsh actually leans.
