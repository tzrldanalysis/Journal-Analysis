## 5. Steel-Man

**Consensus view:** The AI industry's coordinated call to slow frontier development (Amodei, Altman, Musk, Hassabis, all endorsing on Saturday) represents a genuine, industry-led safety intervention in response to accumulating misalignment incidents.

**Steel-man:** Nothing in U.S. or international law currently stops Anthropic from pacing its own development unilaterally — Amodei doesn't need government permission to slow down, yet he's asking for exactly that ("mediate or at least enable"). The timing is difficult to separate from Anthropic's confidential IPO paperwork targeting a ~$2 trillion valuation and up to $100 billion raised, filed the same month; a slowdown that arrives with third-party "employee-level access" evaluators conveniently creates a compliance moat that smaller, later-stage AI entrants can't easily replicate. Sacks explicitly told the CEOs on X to "stop pretending you need anyone else's permission" — an implicit accusation that the government-coordination ask is theater. And Tim Higgins' own B1 column makes the prisoner's-dilemma math plain: if rivals don't follow, Anthropic loses nothing by having proposed it and gains a credibility halo regardless of outcome; if rivals do follow, the frontier leader locks in its lead. The genuine safety concern (rogue-agent incidents at OpenAI, employee estimates of >10% extinction risk) is real, but the proposed remedy — self-selected industry coordination blessed by government — is structurally the same shape as a cartel seeking antitrust cover, which is precisely what the Review & Outlook editorial warns about.
