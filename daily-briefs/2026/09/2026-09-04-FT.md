## 3. Geopolitics & Policy

**Trade, tariffs, and sanctions mechanics.** The Venezuela deal is not a tariff or sanction but a resource-control instrument: the US Treasury takes a 35% stake in Betancourt's Nabep vehicle, with rights to buy 20% of output from 17 oilfields (roughly a fifth of Venezuela's reserves) at production cost and first refusal on the remaining 80%, backed by Pentagon Office of Strategic Capital financial guarantees to investors. Market implication: near-term supply impact is minimal given the state of Venezuela's degraded infrastructure — the FT View itself calls the oil "difficult and expensive to extract." Geopolitical signal: Washington is deliberately routing new investment through a politically connected private operator rather than the state oil company PDVSA, an unconventional resource-control model that congressional Democrats are already calling "an abuse of power." Separately, resumed US-Iran hostilities are pushing oil and gas prices higher, a live retaliation-risk channel running in parallel to the Venezuela play.

**[FT ANGLE] China explicitly rejected US-drafted trade language at a US-hosted G20, a rarer and more pointed signal than a standard tariff dispute.** Beijing objected to communiqué language calling for the elimination of "non-market policies," refusing to sign on even in a multilateral forum it attended in Washington. Mechanism: no new tariff or enforcement action, but a direct diplomatic refusal to accept the US's own framing of what constitutes acceptable industrial policy — a signal that continued friction, not détente, is the likelier path even where both sides are nominally at the same table.

**Institutional and structural dynamics — [FT ANGLE].** Roberto Vannacci's Future Nazionale, now polling at 14%, is described by the European Council on Foreign Relations as a "Trojan horse for Russian influence inside Italy" that could "shatter the EU's collective security consensus" should it join Meloni's governing coalition — a scenario a Meloni deputy foreign minister, Edmondo Cirielli, has already said he would welcome (before the party walked the remark back as "personal opinion"). Vannacci's manifesto calls for reopening channels with Russia, re-evaluating Italy's Nato commitments, and blamed Ukrainian drone strikes on Russian refineries for this summer's petrol-price spike — Kremlin-adjacent framing from a figure whose Moscow posting as Italy's military attaché is on the public record. Separately, Paris and Berlin are pushing to fold the EEAS, the EU's diplomatic service, under European Commission control to find savings — a live institutional-consolidation fight that would reduce the diplomatic service's autonomy just as Vannacci-style domestic pressure tests the bloc's foreign-policy cohesion from the other direction.
