## 3. Geopolitics & Policy

**Trade, tariffs, sanctions.** The U.S.-Canada relationship is the sharpest mechanism story today: 50% U.S. tariffs on Canadian-made vehicles and auto parts take effect Jan. 1 in retaliation for the collapse of trade talks; Canada's counter-tariffs of 50% on roughly 700 U.S. products (appliances to cheese) take effect Sept. 8. Bessent's framing — blaming Carney personally for turning this "into a political shouting match" — signals Washington intends to hold the line rather than de-escalate, and it's happening at the G-20 finance ministers' meeting the U.S. is hosting for the first time since trade tensions strained the relationship. Market implication: auto-sector input costs rise on both sides of the border: geopolitical signal: this reinforces the pattern (also visible in Iceland's EU rejection) of U.S. allies hedging away from dependence on Washington rather than reconciling with it. Separately, Bessent warned of sanctions on countries assisting Iran — with China, Iran's largest trading partner, sitting inside the same G-20 room, that's a direct source of friction baked into this week's summit.

**Institutional and structural dynamics.** The Hormuz mine-strike escalation is the more structurally significant story: Iran firing mines from shore-based rocket launchers is a tactical innovation that sidesteps the U.S. Navy's ability to interdict a mine-laying vessel before it reaches the water. U.S. forces destroyed the launch sites, but the underlying asymmetry — Iran only needs to make transiting the strait feel risky, while the U.S. must guarantee it's safe — remains unresolved. Iran's economy is genuinely strained, with figures like former IRGC commander and parliamentary speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf publicly warning the regime "may not survive economic strangulation," per Walter Russell Mead's column, but Russia and China are actively working to keep Tehran propped up, meeting at this week's Shanghai Cooperation Organisation summit in Kyrgyzstan alongside India — explicitly framed in the piece as a "counterweight to U.S. global influence." Putin, Xi and Modi held bilateral sessions on the sidelines; Putin-Pezeshkian talks are expected Tuesday. The structural signal: Iran's isolation from the West is being actively offset by a non-Western bloc that has its own reasons (sanctions relief, energy pricing leverage) to keep the conflict simmering rather than resolving.

Separately, Russia's jet-powered drone campaign against Kyiv (1,500 long-range drones over four days, ~800 jet-powered, killing 38 in a single ammunition-depot strike) is described by multiple sources as part of a deliberate strategy to wear down Ukrainian civilian resilience rather than achieve battlefield gains — Russian forces have "advanced very little" in Donbas this year. European officials increasingly see Russia's hybrid-warfare campaign (the Leipzig/Halle drone incident, Polish rail cyberattacks, explosions at European defense plants) as reconnaissance for a wider confrontation, not isolated harassment — a view now shared by Poland's foreign minister.
