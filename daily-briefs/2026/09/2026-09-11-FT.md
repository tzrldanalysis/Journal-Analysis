## 5. Steel-Man

## 12. One To Watch

The Benn Steil "Markets Insight" column's argument — that the US has lost its historic base of price-insensitive Treasury buyers, with the share held by central banks and reserve managers falling from 76% to 24% over two decades — deserves more weight than its placement as a single opinion column suggests. It reframes today's entire bond-selloff narrative: not a cyclical Fed-versus-CPI story but a structural, self-reinforcing one in which higher debt issuance itself pushes up the rate demanded by remaining buyers, with a Warsh-led Fed's stated ambition to shrink its balance sheet potentially compounding the problem rather than easing it. If correct, no single CPI print or Fed decision resolves this — it would recur every issuance cycle.
