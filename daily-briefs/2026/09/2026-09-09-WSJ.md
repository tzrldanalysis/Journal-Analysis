GameStop, Salesforce, AstraZeneca and the CHIPS Act quantum names all had strong days or quarters in the same edition — the "threatens stocks' long run" headline describes a real and connected risk, but overstates, on the paper's own numbers, how broadly that risk has actually materialized so far.

## 6. Prior Under Pressure

**Prior:** Corporate software incumbents — Salesforce, Workday, ServiceNow, SAP — are being structurally disintermediated by AI coding tools and agentic commerce, which justified the roughly 30% valuation compression some of these names saw earlier this year.

**Pressure:** Salesforce's beat-and-raise quarter, 22% post-earnings pop, and new Anthropic integration; ServiceNow's and Snowflake's better-than-expected growth; Workday's strong AI-agent adoption results; and Sam Altman's own on-record concession that software disruption "hadn't happened as quickly as he thought it would" all undercut the disintermediation thesis directly. Gartner's Chandrasekaran adds the structural reason why: "most buyers of technology are not engineering organizations — they are operators," and integrating AI-generated code into the tangle of existing enterprise IT is a materially harder problem than generating the code itself. Yet the same day, Shopify — the incumbent that took the disruption thesis most seriously and restructured around it — fell 7% in a "broader software selloff," showing the market hasn't yet resolved which reading is correct. That's a live, unresolved tension the edition documents but doesn't settle.

## 7. What Would Have To Be True

**Implication:** Elevated oil prices persist through H1 2027 without forcing Fed re-tightening or derailing the broader equity rally, consistent with Goldman's and BofA's base-case forecasts.
**Assumption:** The Iran-Houthi-Saudi conflict stays contained to tanker seizures, drone strikes and refinery damage rather than escalating into a full closure of the Strait of Hormuz or a direct, sustained U.S.-Iran military exchange.
**Fragility:** U.S. officials say Iran's recent attempts to strike U.S. Navy vessels are becoming more sophisticated and may be getting tracking assistance from China or Russia. A single successful hit on a U.S. carrier or destroyer — after four consecutive failed attempts — would force an asymmetric U.S. response that could shut Hormuz entirely, invalidating both the banks' base-case oil forecasts and the Fed's current inflation-progress assumptions simultaneously.

**Implication:** Washington's state-directed capital model — CHIPS Act equity stakes, DOE nuclear loans, MP Materials-style floor-price guarantees — successfully builds durable alternative supply chains in quantum, nuclear and critical minerals before the November 10 China-truce expiry forces a decision point.
**Assumption:** These early-stage commitments (individually a few hundred million to low billions of dollars) scale into commercially viable, investable capacity fast enough to matter within the compressed timeline, and China doesn't retaliate faster than the alternative supply chains can absorb the disruption.
**Fragility:** Morrison's own op-ed concedes these measures "haven't yet broken Chinese dominance," and China's June addition of ten more U.S. firms — including MP Materials itself, the centerpiece of the alternative-supply strategy — to its export-control list shows Beijing can directly target the companies the U.S. is trying to build up. The September 24 Xi-Trump meeting could extend the truce or let it lapse seven weeks before the November 10 deadline either way, and the paper offers no read on which outcome is more likely.

## 12. One To Watch

The Consultative Shipping Group's warning that global trade is bifurcating into "two parallel trade systems" — one rules-based, one built on sanctions-evading shadow fleets — is a bigger structural claim than any single tariff fight or even the Iran war itself, yet it received a single paragraph buried inside the Houthi-attack story. If the alliance of 18 shipping nations is right, it implies an emerging institutional split in how the 80% of world trade that moves by sea is insured, flagged and enforced — with consequences current diplomatic and market frameworks aren't built to handle. It's the kind of quiet, technical-sounding warning from an "informal alliance" that tends to be proven right a few years after nobody paid attention to it.
