## 1. Today's Themes

## 5. Steel-Man

**Consensus view:** The bond-market selloff and Bessent's buyback intervention signal fiscal/policy dysfunction — Washington losing control of its own borrowing costs.

**Steel-man:** Joseph Sternberg's opinion column argues the opposite: rising yields reflect *improving* growth expectations (tech companies willingly bidding up rates to fund AI capex) and the long-overdue unwinding of Fed-enabled "financial repression" that let Congress borrow below true market rates since 2008. Under Warsh's no-forward-guidance, shrinking-balance-sheet regime, rates are simply normalizing to reflect real fiscal risk — which is a feature, not a bug, if the goal is disciplining future borrowing. Bessent's buyback, in this reading, isn't a challenge to Warsh's hands-off approach but confirmation it's already biting: a politician's first clumsy attempt to cope with rates no longer artificially suppressed. The bond "chaos" is therefore a sign the mechanism is working as intended, with more painful adjustments — not less — still to come.

## 6. Prior Under Pressure

**Prior:** US Treasurys remain the world's unimpeachable safe-haven asset, commanding a yield discount purely for that status.

**Pressure:** Caballero's and Lustig's research (cited in the Capital Account column) shows Treasurys now trade at a yield *premium* to swaps and, since 2022, no longer yield less than comparable AAA corporates — a reversal of decades of pattern. Post-"Liberation Day" tariffs, stocks and yields moved *together* (both down) rather than the classic flight-to-quality inverse relationship. A $40T debt stock, a $2.1T projected fiscal 2026 deficit (double Bessent's original 3%-of-GDP target), and record $1.9T corporate IG issuance competing for the same capital pool are structural, not cyclical, pressures on the safe-haven thesis.
