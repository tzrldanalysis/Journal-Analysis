## 3. Geopolitics & Policy

**Trade/tariffs/sanctions mechanics.** Trump has threatened to double Canadian auto and parts tariffs to 50% from 25% effective Jan. 1, layered on an active USMCA renegotiation. Mechanism: Canadian-assembled vehicles (Stellantis's Pacifica in Windsor, GM trucks in Oshawa, Ford Super Duty in Oakville) cross the border multiple times as components before final assembly, so a rate increase compounds through the supply chain rather than applying once. Market implication: UBS flags Honda and Toyota as most exposed; automakers are largely treating the Jan. 1 deadline as a negotiating threat rather than planning around it, meaning any actual implementation would be a repricing shock, not a priced-in glide path. Geopolitical signal: Canada retains leverage via retaliatory tariffs on U.S. pickup trucks — its single biggest American export category — which is a credible countermeasure, not a bluff.

Separately, Vietnam has overtaken China, Mexico and Taiwan to record the single largest H1 bilateral trade surplus with the U.S. ($114B, or $123B in gross imports, up 40% YoY), a direct consequence of the 6.5% effective Vietnamese tariff rate versus 23.2% on China (Penn Wharton). This is transshipment-adjacent reshoring at scale — Apple, Nike, Lululemon and smaller importers (TOV Furniture: 25%→60% Vietnam sourcing since 2024) have all shifted sourcing specifically because of the tariff differential, not underlying cost advantage. Vietnam is now structurally exposed to the next round of tariff enforcement precisely because it has become the largest surplus country.

**Institutional/structural dynamics.** The U.S. Postal Service issued new mail-ballot regulations (bar codes, envelope design mandates, a federal voter-registration data submission requirement) days before states including Colorado (Sept. 19 start) and North Carolina (Sept. 4 start) must begin mailing midterm ballots. A federal judge and several states say they believed the agency was barred from doing this by existing court order; the administration disputes that. Roughly two dozen Democratic-led states are now litigating simultaneously against both the original executive order and the new USPS rules, with no resolution likely before ballots must go out — meaning the legal question of which ballots count could be adjudicated after the fact.

Fed governor Lisa Cook's lawyer formally rejected the mortgage-fraud allegations underpinning Trump's renewed attempt to remove her, teeing up a decision by Trump on whether to try again after the Supreme Court required due process. This is a live test of Fed-board independence with direct market relevance given the ongoing Warsh rate-path uncertainty — a contested board seat during a divided-committee period compounds signal noise.
