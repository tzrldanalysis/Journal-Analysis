## 1. Today's Themes

**A tariff refund windfall is doing real work propping up the earnings tape, and it's temporary.** More than 40 S&P 500 companies have booked roughly $9.6 billion in tariff refunds after the Supreme Court struck down Trump's emergency tariffs, with Apple ($2.2B), Nike ($986M), FedEx (~$800M), Amazon ($640M) and GM ($500M) leading. Apple's refund alone added 11 cents a share — about 5% of the quarter's total EPS. Customs has accepted $128.7 billion in refund claims for processing. This is a one-time boost layered on top of otherwise-decent results, and Tapestry's own guidance flags the mechanism explicitly: a "modest benefit" in H1 that "will turn into a headwind" in H2.

**Stocks are compounding faster than the economy that's supposed to justify them — and the widening gap is now a named phenomenon.** Greg Ip's Capital Account column documents a "jobless economic boom": real GDP growth of ~2.1% over the past year against S&P 500 median earnings growth that's accelerated to 13% from 8% two years ago, while total payrolls shrank in July for the fifth time in the past 12 months and private payroll growth has averaged only ~50,000/month. The S&P 500 closed at its 27th record of the year (7798.99, +0.65%) the same day the jobless-claims report showed claims rising to 209,000, above forecasts. Soft PPI (flat in July vs. +0.2% expected) and cooling CPI are being read as room for a rate cut, dragging the 10-year yield down to 4.64% from a two-year high of 4.745% on July 31 — even as a Fed governance fight over Kevin Warsh's credibility plays out in the letters pages.

**AI infrastructure financing is migrating off balance sheet and off the grid, diffusing credit risk into a layer investors can't easily see.** SpaceX is building its own natural-gas plant in Texas; Musk has personally bought a gas-turbine manufacturer; Fermi (co-founded by Rick Perry) just landed its first customer, TensorWave, for 222 megawatts under a "Bring Your Own Power" structure where Fermi isn't required to fund or issue debt for construction but can buy the plant later. A McKinsey survey finds 65% of power-industry respondents plan on-site generation, two-thirds of them via gas. This is now systemically large — Cleanview tracks 59 off-grid data-center projects with ~90 gigawatts of combined capacity, enough to power Texas on its hottest day — and it sits outside the balance sheets analysts are used to scrutinizing.
