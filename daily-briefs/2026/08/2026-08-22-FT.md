## 6. Prior Under Pressure

**Prior:** US Treasuries and the dollar remain the default global safe haven virtually regardless of US fiscal conditions, because there is no credible alternative reserve asset.

**Pressure:** Today's paper documents Treasuries and the dollar weakening simultaneously — an unusual combination that read-throughs to a genuine confidence problem rather than routine relative-value repricing, since a normal risk-off move would see the dollar strengthen even as yields rise. Gold, bitcoin and the Swiss franc are all rallying as explicit alternatives ("debasement trade"), with gold on track for its best month since 1999. The bond-market insiders' interpretation of Bessent's yen intervention — that it exists to stop Japan selling Treasuries to defend its own currency — implies foreign official-sector demand for Treasuries is itself now a policy-management problem rather than an assumption. Layered onto this is the $40tn national-debt milestone reached the same week, with the CBO projecting debt held by the public will exceed the post-WWII high of 106% of GDP by decade's end and hit 120% by 2036. The combination of foreign-holder anxiety, twin-asset weakness, and a Treasury that has now intervened in three separate markets in three weeks strains — without yet breaking — the assumption that dollar-and-Treasury safe-haven status is structurally immune to US fiscal deterioration.

## 7. What Would Have To Be True

**Implication:** Europe's low-debt bloc (Germany, the Nordics, the Baltics, Poland) converts its fiscal advantage into real Nato leadership, nuclear-technology sharing, and UN Security Council influence over the coming cycle.

**Assumption:** Fiscal capacity must translate into coordinated political will and institutional bargaining power, rather than being absorbed helping bail out high-debt peers (UK, France, Italy) without extracting concessions in return.

**Fragility:** A recession, banking shock, or new supply crisis could force the low-debt bloc into its own defensive fiscal posture before it has consolidated leverage. More fundamentally, legacy power structures — Britain and France's permanent UNSC seats, France's independent nuclear deterrent — are treaty-based and historically sticky; fiscal strength alone has not historically been sufficient to force their renegotiation, and Germany's own defence-spending effectiveness is already being questioned by markets (Rheinmetall -22% YTD, KNDS's delayed IPO), suggesting the fiscal-to-military-capacity conversion this thesis requires is not yet visible in either equity or corporate financing data.

**Implication:** China's state-directed reallocation of capital into "hard technology" (Star Market listings, humanoid robotics, semiconductors) successfully builds a self-sufficient tech ecosystem that insulates it from further US export controls.

**Assumption:** Onshore capital markets can fund R&D at sufficient scale to close the "ChatGPT moment" gap in humanoid-robot autonomy — estimated by industry executives themselves at anywhere from two to ten years away — and that semiconductor self-sufficiency can be achieved even as the US permits only "small batches" of secondary-tier H200 chips into China.

**Fragility:** Unitree founder Wang Xingxing and HSBC's Corey Chan both flag that current AI models lack sufficient training data and parameter scale (most run 4bn-7bn parameters; useful generalised-task competence may require hundreds of billions) — a genuine technical bottleneck, not just a financing one. If the breakthrough timeline slips toward the pessimistic end of the range while Star 50 valuations (+23% YTD) already price optimism, momentum capital could reprice sharply on disappointment, independent of any US policy change.
