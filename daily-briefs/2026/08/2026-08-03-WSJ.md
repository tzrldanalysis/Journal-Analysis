## 1. Today's Themes

**AI capital expenditure is bifurcating into a two-tier market, and the tier line is "do you rent out a cloud."**
Amazon (AWS +37% revenue, 39% operating margin), Microsoft (Azure +43% revenue) and Alphabet (cloud +82% revenue) were rewarded with the largest one-day market-cap gains in history — Microsoft alone added roughly $450 billion in a single session, and Microsoft plus Amazon added a combined ~$950 billion since reporting. Meta (-8% on guidance to negative free cash flow in H2) and Apple (-7.4%, worst day since the tariff turmoil) were punished on the same earnings week. The distinguishing variable investors are now pricing is not AI spend itself but whether that spend generates an external, contracted revenue stream (AWS's 10-year, $100+ billion Anthropic contract is the template) versus internal cost absorption. This connects directly to the Streetwise column's own warning that "the increasing use of debt financing means that if AI more broadly turns out to be a bubble, it could hit the financial system" — the same edition that celebrates cloud vindication also flags the leverage building underneath it.

**Fed credibility is fracturing along the hawkish-yield/dovish-chair axis.**
New chairman Kevin Warsh's press conference — held while holding rates at 3.50%-3.75% — was read by analysts as implying that elevated bond yields have already done the Fed's tightening for it. Bond traders answered with a sell-off: the 30-year Treasury yield hit a 19-year high and the 10-year hit an 18-month high (now 4.743%). Analyst notes were literally titled "Doved and Confused." Warsh is also weighing fewer scheduled policy meetings, which would reduce the number of formal touchpoints markets have with the Fed precisely when his signaling is already being second-guessed.

OPEC+ raised output for a sixth straight month regardless, completing the unwind of 1.65 million b/d in voluntary cuts and setting up a contested 2027 quota-baseline review.

Cross-reference: NYC's pied-à-terre tax rollout (960,000 properties flagged for a levy meant to hit ~10,000) is the live case study in what messy wealth-tax administration looks like — directly relevant to California voters weighing a similar measure in November.

**[WEAK SIGNAL] AI is quietly dissolving the employer-employee unit at the same time it's raising fiduciary-liability questions no one has priced.**
A solo entrepreneur is running a $10 million-revenue AI company with zero staff; Stripe data shows information-sector new-business applications up ~45% while hiring intent in that sector fell the most of any measured industry.
