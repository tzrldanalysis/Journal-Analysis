## 3. Geopolitics & Policy

**Solar/polysilicon tariffs.** Trump signed a proclamation imposing a 15% tariff on imported solar-energy materials and equipment, effective Dec. 4, 2026, plus minimum import prices on polysilicon, solar panels and components. Companies building U.S. manufacturing can apply for exemptions; a first-sale certification requirement targets transshipment underpricing. Commerce Secretary Lutnick framed it explicitly as price-setting to block Chinese dumping. Market implication: higher input costs for utility-scale solar developers; geopolitical signal: extends the Section 232 tariff logic already applied to lumber, steel, aluminum and copper into a new critical-minerals-adjacent front against Chinese manufacturing dominance — the same dominance documented in today's Dongguan reporting on China becoming "a factory for factories."

**Softwood lumber tariffs.** A 10% tariff layered on existing antidumping/countervailing duties brings the combined rate to roughly 25%. Benchmark lumber prices hit a four-year high ($535 per thousand board feet) even as home sales slump, filtering tariff costs directly into home-building expenses mid-affordability-crisis, ahead of November's midterms.

**Strait of Hormuz mechanics.** Mediators are finalizing a weekend framework dividing traffic into an inbound lane near Iran and an outbound lane near Oman, in exchange for sanctions waivers on oil sales and release of frozen funds. The IRGC is demanding more explicit acknowledgment of Iran's role and clearer benefits before signing off, while hardline commentators call any deal "an escape route for the enemy." Retaliation risk is not theoretical: Iran has already struck Saudi tankers and conducted a drone attack on an Egyptian port during this conflict.

**Gaza/Hamas.** The Board of Peace's July 31 disarmament road map is fraying — Israel resumed strikes it frames as a response to Hamas's non-disarmament; Turkey, Egypt and Qatar issued a joint statement condemning the strikes as a breach. Ten months after the war's formal end, no Hamas fighter has surrendered a weapon and no Hamas signature exists on the new road map. This threatens Trump's stated foreign-policy win ahead of the midterms and while the Iran talks continue in parallel.

**Hezbollah/Lebanon.** Israel intensified strikes in southern Lebanon after accusing Hezbollah of a ceasefire violation (an IED killed two Israeli soldiers), complicating a seventh round of U.S.-brokered Israel-Lebanon talks. Both governments still want Hezbollah disarmed, but officials privately acknowledge it can't be forced militarily — only a political agreement, still absent, resolves it.

**NATO/Russia.** New U.S. intelligence assessments place a possible limited Russian test of an allied country's resolve — cyberattack to small-scale land incursion — in the fall-2026-to-2029 window, explicitly tied to the same interceptor stockpiles drawn down by the Iran war and Ukraine transfers. The scenario's likelihood is assessed as low but rising with time.

**Birthright citizenship.** Trump signed narrower executive orders after the Supreme Court's June ruling voided his broader 2025 order — this pair targets "birth tourism" specifically, screening tourist, student and work-visa applicants and threatening lifetime entry bans. Sen. Schumer has vowed a court challenge; the institutional signal is a White House testing process-based workarounds after a Supreme Court loss rather than reversing policy direction.

**Media/statistical-agency institutional dynamics.** The FCC's 2-1 repeal of the 39% ownership cap sets up a fresh Congress-vs-agency authority fight (the cap was set by statute in 2004). Sen. Warren's letter to Commerce over the BEA's PCE methodology change raises the same institutional-credibility question one step removed — whether executive-controlled statistical agencies can be trusted to measure the economy the Fed and markets rely on.
