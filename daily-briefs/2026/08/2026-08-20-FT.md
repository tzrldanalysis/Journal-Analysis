## 1. Today's Themes

**The Iran war's cost is now being paid in shipping and diesel, not crude price.**
Brent is up only modestly (four consecutive days of gains to $92.35, +1.5%), but the physical transmission channel has exploded: US diesel hit $5.47/gallon, close to the all-time high of $5.82, with the diesel-crude "crack spread" at a record; Gulf-to-Asia tanker rates hit $15.22/barrel (highest since Argus began tracking in 2005); Panama Canal transit slots and Black Sea tanker rates are at records; Rhine barge rates to Cologne/Duisburg/Frankfurt are at their highest since 2012. Argus's John Ollett calls it "the single greatest disruption the shipping market has seen on record, eclipsing the Covid pandemic and Russia sanctions." Cross-reference: this is the same shock driving European households to save more than at any point since the pandemic (14 of 18 European countries above pre-pandemic savings rates) and is embedded in the US Treasury's decision to double long-bond buybacks as the 30-year yield hit its highest since 2007.

**Private credit's opacity problem is compounding into a credibility crisis, and pension giants are leaning in anyway.**
The Mark Walter/TWG federal investigation (insurers making $20bn+ in undisclosed related-party loans), a Guggenheim insurance unit's separate disclosure of $20bn in unidentified related-party assets, MBS Group's seizure by BlackRock's HPS and Oaktree after default, Blue Owl's slowest fundraising in three years, and Ares halving a continuation vehicle after investor pushback on valuations all land in one edition — alongside an explicit FT View editorial ("Private credit risks remain at large") and Fitch's record 6% direct-lending default rate. Cross-reference: on the same pages, Canada's CPPIB is *expanding* its reliance on KKR, Blackstone and EQT for infrastructure megadeals, the exact model under scrutiny.

**Middle powers and mid-cap challengers are hedging away from single-point dependency, across every unrelated domain in today's edition.**
Kazakhstan is courting both Chinese and US capital specifically to avoid being "beholden" to either power (Citic CLSA's Max Hopkins); Google is diversifying its TPU supply chain away from sole reliance on its own design teams via a $12.2bn Marvell warrant; Germany's AfD is deliberately dialling back its public Maga alignment ahead of state elections; e-sims are eroding travellers' lock-in to incumbent mobile roaming deals; Turkey is asserting an independent Syria policy against Israeli objections. [WEAK SIGNAL] The same dynamic shows up quietly in policy pages too — Singapore's carried-interest tax cuts (responding to Hong Kong), Mexico's Codi digital-payments push, and Vietnam's SME-versus-chaebol debate are all mid-sized economies actively re-engineering the rules to capture mobile capital and talent rather than passively accepting existing flows.

**Iran is signalling deliberate geographic escalation into Europe, and European households are already pricing it in.**
FT insiders report Iran has assessed strikes on Bulgaria's Bezmer air base (approved for US refuelling last month) and Cyprus's British base (already drone-struck in March), plus subsea cables in the Strait of Hormuz. One insider's quote is explicit: "This was also a message to Europe: it can receive missiles, so it should know where it should stand in this war." Cross-reference: this sits alongside the Turkey-Israel clash over the Abu al-Duhur base in Syria (Nato's second-biggest army versus Israel) and the European savings data — both NATO cohesion and household behaviour are already adjusting to a wider-war scenario that hasn't happened yet.

## 2. Finance & Markets

**Rates.** The US Treasury said it would "at least double" buybacks of 10-20yr and 20-30yr paper (from $2bn to at least $4bn), explicitly to relieve "upward pressure on long-dated yields" after the 30-year hit almost 5.34% on Tuesday — the highest since 2007. The announcement triggered an immediate rally: 30-year yield down 9bp to 5.19%, 10-year down 5-6bp to 4.65-4.66%. A same-day $16bn 20-year auction drew "pretty middle of the road" demand at 5.204%, a small premium to the 5.199% secondary yield (TD Securities' Gennadiy Goldberg). UK gilts rallied in sympathy (30-year -5bp to 5.79%, 2-year -2bp to 4.36%) on soft UK inflation pass-through data. The credit dimension: Deutsche Bank's George Saravelos flagged that "the more these [buybacks] are perceived as distortionary to market pricing, the more the dollar is likely to weaken" — the dollar fell 0.7-0.8% on the announcement.

**Credit.** Private credit stress is now a multi-story pattern rather than an isolated event: Fitch's record 6% direct-lending default rate (year to July); the Walter/TWG federal probe into insurers' undisclosed related-party loans exceeding $20bn; a separate Guggenheim insurance-unit disclosure of $20bn+ in unidentified related-party assets; MBS Group (Hollywood's largest soundstage-equipment supplier, serving Netflix and Warner Bros Discovery) seized by BlackRock's HPS and Oaktree after default; Blue Owl's slowest fundraising pace in three years following heavy withdrawal requests; Ares scaling down a billion-dollar continuation vehicle by more than half on investor valuation pushback. The FT View editorial states the central question plainly: "if there were a serious downturn, the question turns to who is on the hook" — and warns that circular ownership between private capital firms "can further amplify risk."

**FX.** The Kazakh tenge is Europe/Asia's best-performing currency in 2026, up 9.7% against the dollar, driven by 16.75% policy rates (even after two cuts from 18%) and new Euroclear connectivity for domestic bonds — Goldman's Clemens Grafe notes the appeal is capital-flow-driven, not an oil-price play, since most Kazakh oil profit accrues to foreign operators. Euro +0.8% to $1.1670; sterling +0.6% to $1.3610 — both largely a mirror of the Treasury-buyback dollar move.

**Commodities.** Gold jumped 3.7% to $4,494.29/oz on the buyback announcement and dollar weakness. Brent rose for a fourth straight session to $92.35 (+1.5%) on scepticism a Strait of Hormuz deal is close. Diesel at $5.47/gallon, nearing the $5.82 all-time high, with crack spreads at records — Gulf Oil's Tom Kloza: "these are body punches to the middle of the economy."

**Equities.** S&P 500 +0.33% to 7716.88; Nasdaq +0.35% after an earlier chip-stock sell-off. Standouts: Moderna (MRNA) more than doubled (+144%) on melanoma-vaccine trial results with Merck, taking its market cap to $61bn and making it the S&P 500's second-best 2026 performer behind Sandisk; BioNTech +20% on the read-through; Merck +10% to a new high. Carlsberg -2% despite alcohol-free volumes +11% globally, after missing its own cost-savings timeline (roughly half of a targeted £110mn achieved versus a 30-40% expectation).
