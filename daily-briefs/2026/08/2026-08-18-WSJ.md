## 5. Steel-Man

**Consensus view:** The AI capital-expenditure supercycle is a durable, self-financing growth engine that justifies record hyperscaler valuations and record industrial demand (Caterpillar, Cummins, semiconductors).

**Steel-man:** The WSJ's own investigative piece today shows the AI buildout is increasingly financed through $3 trillion in off-balance-sheet leases and purchase commitments — nearly triple the reported capex figure and growing far faster than revenue — structured specifically so that individual companies' balance sheets don't show the true leverage (Morgan Stanley's own accounting analysts flag this as a transparency problem). Harvard's Willy Shih explicitly compares the current data-center build-out to the late-1990s fiber-optic glut that left assets stranded for 20 years, and economists across multiple articles today (Cummins, Caterpillar coverage) flag overshoot risk. If demand assumptions prove wrong, the obligation structure — designed to be off-balance-sheet rather than absent — doesn't disappear; it becomes exactly the kind of hidden, correlated leverage that turned a single-manager AI hedge fund's implosion into a Street-wide margin-call event in July.

## 7. What Would Have To Be True

**Implication 1:** The AI infrastructure capex boom continues to lift industrial and semiconductor earnings (Caterpillar, Cummins, Nvidia) without triggering a credit event.

**Assumption:** Actual end-demand for AI compute (training and inference revenue) grows fast enough, and consistently enough, to justify $3 trillion in off-balance-sheet lease and purchase commitments across nine companies before those obligations convert to cash lease payments and on-balance-sheet debt.

**Fragility:** A repeat of the cheap-open-source-model shock that triggered Situational Awareness's July unwind — or any sustained pause in hyperscaler capex guidance — would strand data-center capacity the way 1990s fiber-optic overbuild did, and unlike that era, today's obligations are far more concentrated among a handful of interlinked counterparties (Nvidia, OpenAI, SoftBank/SB Energy, Meta, Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon), raising correlation risk in any unwind.

**Implication 2:** Economic pressure (oil-export collapse, sanctions) eventually forces Iran back to a durable negotiated settlement that reopens Hormuz and de-escalates the regional war.

**Assumption:** Iran's leadership calculates that further economic deterioration is more costly than continued "salami-slicing" escalation, and that U.S./Gulf-state resolve to sustain the pressure campaign (rather than fracture, as the Oman episode suggests) remains intact through the 2026 midterms.

**Fragility:** Today's reporting shows the opposite dynamic building — hard-liners have consolidated control of security institutions (Vahidi, Rezaei, Taeb appointments), rebuilt missile production faster than expected, and internal Iranian voices are on record describing the current lull as "the calm before the storm." If Iran's calculus is that time favors escalation rather than capitulation, the entire "sanctions eventually work" thesis embedded in current oil-price and equity-risk-premium assumptions is misspecified.
