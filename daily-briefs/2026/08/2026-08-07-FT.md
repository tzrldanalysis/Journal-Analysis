But Iran's deputy foreign minister explicitly said the deal "does not mean the full reopening of the Strait of Hormuz," and the US naval blockade on Iranian ports stays in place until Tehran's supreme leader signs off — who has "still to approve the deal." **China is tightening the outbound-capital net around its own citizens, and Hong Kong financials are the first visible casualty.** Beijing applied a 20% tax on dividends/interest from offshore insurance policies (per Caixin), triggering AIA (-5.9%), Prudential HK (-4.6%, after -6.4% in London the prior day), FWD (-5.6%) and Manulife (-2.1%). This follows last month's sweeping 20%-rate rules on assets moved into offshore trusts and an FT report this week that Beijing has launched a "global hunt" for decades of unpaid taxes from wealthy nationals. Hong Kong insurers wrote a record $42bn in new life business last year, largely funded by mainland savers using policies to move money outside capital controls — that channel is now visibly closing.

**European industrial capital is voting with its feet toward North America even as European defence spending booms domestically — the two flows are not the same money.** Saint-Gobain (Bazin) is pushing North American revenue share from 22% toward "closer to 30%," having already shifted profit contribution from ~60% Europe to roughly a third each Europe/North America; the company has "anticipated... the de-globalisation of the world of today" by localising production. The signal: European industrials are diversifying revenue *away* from Europe even as they profit from Europe's own security build-out — a hedge against the durability of that build-out.

And El-Erian argues the "dot plot" Warsh suspended was itself a source of "false precision" that the market is now grieving out of "behavioural inertia," not genuine analytical necessity. If this is right, the sell-off is a transition cost of legitimate reform, not evidence the reform is failing.

## 6. Prior Under Pressure

**Prior:** Central bank forward guidance and dot-plot transparency are necessary preconditions for market stability and low borrowing costs.

**Pressure:** Warsh has stripped both away deliberately, and while yields rose, inflation *expectations* (the actual mandate-relevant variable) fell over the same period. If that divergence holds through September, it directly challenges the two-decade consensus that opacity is destabilising — the Warsh experiment may end up demonstrating the opposite of what markets currently assume it's demonstrating. The five-yearly "framework review" cycle itself (2020, 2025) is also under implicit pressure: El-Erian frames both reviews as having been "too narrow and overly backward-looking," suggesting the entire institutional self-review mechanism, not just guidance policy, is due for a rethink.

## 13. Research Call Questions

**1.** Warsh's opacity strategy is producing a bond sell-off even as inflation expectations (swaps-implied) fall — a divergence El-Erian frames as proof the strategy is working, not failing. **If inflation swaps and realized yields continue to diverge through September, does that validate reduced-guidance central banking as a durable global model, or is this a US-specific artifact of Trump-era Fed distrust that won't travel to other central banks?**
