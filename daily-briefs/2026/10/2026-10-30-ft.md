## 3. Geopolitics & Policy

**Trade/sanctions mechanics.** The Trump administration's general licence exempting Russian diesel from sanctions (announced October 9, in effect now) has let at least three tankers — roughly 140,000 tonnes combined, including vessels sanctioned by the EU, UK and Canada — approach US ports just after next week's midterms. New Jersey Governor Mikie Sherrill is "evaluating options" to physically block delivery; Senate Democrats (Blumenthal) argue the licence conflicts with the 2022 Ending Importation of Russian Oil Act and have written to shipping companies demanding tanker recalls; Republican Senator Thom Tillis has also warned US businesses of investigation risk. Market implication: near-term diesel price relief ahead of winter, timed to blunt a key midterm cost-of-living line of attack. Geopolitical signal: Washington is now trading sanctions-enforcement credibility for domestic fuel-price optics, a move Ukraine-aligned voices call corrosive to alliance trust — a second-order cost not currently priced into the diesel market.

**Second priority — institutional dynamics.** [FT ANGLE] The EU-Canada "associate membership" dispute — von der Leyen's proposal irritated Berlin and other capitals specifically because it risks complicating Ukraine's own accession track and setting a precedent other non-members (Norway, Switzerland) could invoke. This is a distinctly Brussels-institutional read that a US-centric paper would be unlikely to foreground: the story isn't "EU-Canada trade deal" but "EU enlargement architecture under strain from ad hoc bilateral fixes." Separately, the ECB presidency contest is now entangled with a four-way leadership reshuffle (Lagarde, Schnabel, Lane all departing in sequence) at a moment when France and Italy together hold a blocking minority over the appointment — meaning Eurozone fiscal-politics risk (France's budget trajectory) is now directly load-bearing for the bloc's monetary-policy leadership transition, not a side issue.

**Copper tariffs.** The potential for new US copper tariffs has "created artificial tightness in the market for the rest of the world," per Macquarie — a trade-policy distortion now embedded in a commodity price that is simultaneously being questioned on AI-demand grounds, a geopolitical overlay on top of an already-fragile demand narrative.
