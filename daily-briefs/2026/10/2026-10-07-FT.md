## 3. Geopolitics & Policy

Mechanically, the pact is weaker than the headline: Turkey's parliament has not ratified it and cannot deploy troops until it does, Pakistan is treating deployment details as "a matter of operational confidentiality," and one Middle East Institute analyst says Turkey's contribution is more likely to be "military capabilities" than ground troops. Market implication: sustained risk premium on Gulf shipping (freight at $1.3mn/day) and insurance costs. Geopolitical signal: this is a Sunni-majority mutual-defence architecture forming with combined forces of 1.14mn personnel — **[FT ANGLE]** the FT frames this less as reassurance of the existing US-anchored Gulf security order and more as a structural hedge by Riyadh, Islamabad and Ankara against reliance on Washington, a reading a US-centric outlet would likely downplay in favour of an Iran-threat/US-backing narrative.

China's offshore-trust tax (effective October 22, retroactive to 2023-2025) is not a tariff but functions as an extraterritorial capital-control mechanism: a flat 20% levy on dividend/interest income from offshore trusts, with a further 20% exit tax on inheritance or loss of mainland tax residency. Market implication: accelerating capital migration from Hong Kong/Singapore (which together hold roughly $2.9tn+ of mainland offshore wealth) toward New York, where non-resident foreigners pay no capital-gains tax and the US sits outside the OECD's Common Reporting Standard. Geopolitical signal: Beijing is asserting sovereign reach over diaspora wealth regardless of jurisdiction — Barclays calls it a possible first step toward broader estate/inheritance taxation — directly testing Hong Kong and Singapore's claimed autonomy as financial centres.

**Institutional and structural dynamics.** China's decision to scale back December's Apec summit — moving the ministerial meeting online and dropping the traditional leaders' "retreat" — is a live case of multilateral-forum erosion at precisely the moment Beijing is trying to position itself as the adult in the room on multilateralism (explicitly contrasted in the piece with "the US has turned its back on international institutions"). **[FT ANGLE]** The FT treats this as reputationally self-defeating for Beijing's stated ambitions rather than simply another US-China rivalry data point — a structural-credibility angle a US outlet centred on the Trump-Xi summit subplot would likely miss. Germany's AfD winning its first regional-parliament presidency (Saxony-Anhalt) via secret cross-party votes marks a concrete breach of the established "firewall" all mainstream German parties have maintained; CDU leader Thorsten Frei called the unexplained votes "a grave concern." **[FT ANGLE]** This is read in London/Brussels terms as a structural risk to Merz's coalition stability and EU cohesion, not merely a domestic German politics story.
