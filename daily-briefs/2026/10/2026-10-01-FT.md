## 5. Steel-Man

**Consensus view:** AI infrastructure spending is a self-funding virtuous cycle — hyperscaler capex drives chip demand, chip demand drives revenue, revenue services the debt, and equity markets are right to treat this as structurally bullish.

**Steel-man:** Today's own paper supplies the counter-evidence. KKR explicitly warns that reported AI-linked IG exposure (6.3% of the index, more than double the 29-year historical sector average) *understates* the real number once off-balance-sheet leases and credit guarantees are counted — meaning the market cannot currently price what it cannot see. Oracle-linked data-centre loans are "under strain" from construction delays well before any demand shock has even occurred. Bill Gross's column states flatly that the $1tn of 2027 AI investment is "likely to be funded by debt alone now that positive cash flow has disappeared" — directly contradicting the self-funding premise. And South Korea's Kospi — the most AI-levered major index — just had its worst quarter among major markets (-18.8%) on the unwind of a single leveraged hedge fund's bet on the exact trade (Korean memory chips) the bull case depends on. The virtuous-cycle thesis survives only if financing conditions stay benign; today's paper shows three separate cracks in that assumption appearing simultaneously.

## 6. Prior Under Pressure

**Pressure:** France's spread over Germany hit a post-2008 high above 4.8%, and — for the first time since 2012 — its spread over *Italy* flipped positive above 1.2 points, meaning the market now charges France more to borrow than the former "debt hotspot." This is not a single data point — it is a downgrade, a spread inversion against a historical periphery name, and a top-five asset manager's public language all converging in one session.
