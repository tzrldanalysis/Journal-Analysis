**Steel-man:** Today's own reporting undercuts this. Financing has visibly shifted away from hyperscaler balance sheets toward debt-dependent "neo-cloud" providers like CoreWeave, which just lost a quarter of its market value in a week as investors repriced counterparty risk; one executive in the data-centre market told the FT plainly, "it's much harder today to finance a data centre." This isn't a rate-driven wobble in an otherwise sound structure; it's the market beginning to price execution risk in a buildout that was never collectively underwritten against realistic financing assumptions.

## 9. Europe Watch

**France.** The OAT-Bund spread sits at 1.39 percentage points (France 10-year at 4.80 per cent) as the country absorbs what officials call "unprecedented" riot violence: roughly 2,000 arrests nationwide (855 in Paris alone, a one-day record for the city), 600 police and firefighters injured, and an estimated 550,000 demonstrators at the peaceful protests that were hijacked by organised, "hyper-organised" rioters. The unrest lands six months before a presidential election and directly on top of Macron's government trying to pass a budget amid bond-market doubts about its deficit trajectory — Prime Minister Lecornu is being criticised for policing failures by mayors across multiple cities, while Le Pen (Rassemblement National) and Mélenchon (La France Insoumise) trade blame in a way that suggests no governing majority capable of delivering fiscal credibility is currently available. This is a genuine feedback loop: fiscal retrenchment pressure generates unrest, unrest generates political instability, political instability widens the spread that generated the retrenchment pressure in the first place.

**Broader European.** Germany's far-right AfD gains (first-ever state-election seat-loss for the CDU, in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern since 1945) are explicitly cited by Commerzbank's, Axa's and Thyssenkrupp's chief executives as a reason investors are questioning whether chancellor Merz can deliver the up to €4.1tn in investment Germany needs by 2035 (€1tn energy, €700bn infrastructure, €600bn defence) — even as FDI into Germany rose 50 per cent to €86bn last year. Belgium's Euroclear exposure to frozen Russian assets is a live Eurozone financial-stability question the ECB has so far declined to take direct responsibility for. And the EU-US spectrum dispute over Starlink adds a transatlantic trade-and-security dimension to Europe's push for technological "sovereignty."
