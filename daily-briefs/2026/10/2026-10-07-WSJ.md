## 6. Prior Under Pressure

**Prior:** Advanced-economy, G7 sovereign debt is a categorically safer asset class than periphery or emerging-market debt — "core" eurozone and US government bonds are the risk-free anchor investors can rely on regardless of domestic politics.

**Pressure:** France — a G7 nation and founding EU member — now borrows more expensively than Greece or Italy, with an OAT-Bund spread wider than at any point during the 2010-12 eurozone crisis. Its central-bank governor calls the situation a "gradual stranglehold," not a transient event, and traditional stabilizing buyers (the Bank of France itself, Japanese asset managers, hedge funds) have all stepped back simultaneously. The WSJ's own editorial board uses France explicitly to warn that the US (100% debt/GDP) is not structurally different, only earlier in the same cycle. If a founding core-EU sovereign can lose yield primacy over clear periphery names while both of its leading presidential candidates campaign on *more* spending, the "safe core-sovereign" prior is measurably harder to hold with the same confidence it carried a year ago.
