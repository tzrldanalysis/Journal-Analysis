## 1. Today's Themes

**The Fed's rate hold is a political tell, not proof of political immunity.** The FOMC kept rates at 3.75-4 per cent in a 10-2 vote, with governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed's Beth Hammack dissenting in favour of an immediate quarter-point hike — this just a month after the Fed hiked for the first time in over three years. September CPI came in at 3.7 per cent year-on-year and PPI at 6.3 per cent, both worse than expected, and officials signalled they are likely to raise again before year-end. The decision landed days before US midterms that will decide control of Congress. The Fed insists politics plays no role, but holding steady exactly when the data argues loudest for a hike, with fuel prices accelerating on the Iran war, is itself evidence of the constraint it denies. [FT ANGLE]: the FT frames this as a structural credibility story (five-and-a-half years below the 2 per cent PCE target) rather than the horse-race framing a US outlet would likely use.

**France's bond stress is being told as a technical story, not a fiscal one, by the people who'd be blamed either way.** French 10-year yields hit 5 per cent last week, a quarter-century high, and the OAT-Bund spread has roughly doubled over three months to just above 1.1 points. Finance minister Roland Lescure told the FT the pressure was "mostly linked with the unwinding of carry trades" rather than fundamentals — an unusually candid admission from an official with every incentive to blame fiscal consolidation politics instead. Moody's declined to downgrade France despite expectations it would. The contagion read-through is explicit: Italy's spread versus Bunds, while still below France's, has "roughly doubled" too, and Meloni's government is leaning on the EU's deficit escape clause to keep investors calm.

This is the most significant Arab-Israeli electoral showing in the country's history and it still may not translate into governing power. Cross-reference: the same paper's Serbia editorial on the "power of the ballot box" throws this into relief — a decisive protest vote that still can't easily convert into government.

**The Iran war's primary transmission channel into markets and politics is now energy, not direct conflict coverage.** Dutch gas jumped 5 per cent to above €100/MWh — a level last seen during the 2022 post-invasion crisis — on fears of fresh US strikes after the midterms and a cold European winter. This single channel is doing double duty: it is squeezing Iran domestically, feeding the Fed's inflation dilemma, and setting the tone for European gas storage going into winter.
