## 3. Geopolitics & Policy

**Trade/sanctions mechanics.** The U.S. reimposed its Strait of Hormuz blockade in July and has layered fresh sanctions on Iran this summer, successfully stranding roughly 20 Iranian/Iranian-linked tankers off Sri Lanka and Malaysia — crews are now short of food, fuel and water, and Sri Lankan port authorities and ship-repair/provisioning firms are refusing service under threat of U.S. secondary sanctions. Market implication: oil analysts expect the remaining Iranian cargo to clear by year-end, which is a genuine (if narrow) bullish forced-seller event for the shadow-fleet trade, but it also raises counterparty risk for any shipping, insurance or repair firm that touches these vessels. Geopolitical signal: this is sanctions enforcement doing what diplomacy hasn't — Iran's top national-security official is now threatening retaliation against any country (UAE named explicitly) that cooperates with the blockade, raising escalation risk for Gulf aviation and energy infrastructure.

**Mexico's anti-money-laundering overhaul**, passed under direct U.S./FATF pressure to avoid a "gray list" designation, tightens due-diligence and beneficial-ownership disclosure. Market implication: gray-listed countries see investment pull back as "higher-risk jurisdictions" — avoiding that outcome matters for Mexico's bank-funding costs and nearshoring investment thesis, but experts note corrupt-official capture of enforcement means execution risk remains high even with the new law on paper.

**Institutional/structural: the Iraq withdrawal completes a 23-year, $1.7 trillion U.S. military commitment** into a vacuum that Iran-backed militias (Kataib Hezbollah, Asaib Ahl al-Haq, Kata'ib Sayyid Al-Shuhada) are positioned to fill rather than disarm — Wednesday's Trump-set disarmament deadline passed with militia leaders publicly rejecting it ("these weapons are for our sovereignty, our freedom"), and the Pentagon's own Inspector General says counterterrorism cooperation has "slowed to a near halt" because of the Iran war. What changed structurally: the U.S. has gone from occupying power to an embassy-protection posture only, while Iran's IRGC-built militia infrastructure — documented since the 2003 invasion — remains the dominant armed actor. This is a direct, if unstated, rebuttal to the administration's framing that ending the war freed up the military; it instead hands Tehran the local monopoly on force in a top-five global oil producer.

**Canada's Mark Carney is converting confrontation with Trump into a governing mandate** — 60%+ preferred-PM polling, a fast-tracked infrastructure/environmental-review bill, and explicit diplomatic diversification toward China, India and Saudi Arabia. Structural signal: a G7 economy is now actively hedging away from U.S. economic integration as a matter of declared strategy ("we must pursue diminished reliance on the United States" — former PM Harper), which is a capital-flows and supply-chain data point for anyone modeling North American integration as durable.

## 6. Prior Under Pressure

**Prior:** Markets and policymakers widely assumed, in the years after 2008-09, that the world had entered a durable low-inflation, low-rate regime — and that even after the Covid inflation shock, rates would eventually normalize back toward that old equilibrium.

**Pressure:** The 10-year closed at its highest level since 2002 — above the pre-crisis 2007 peak — on its largest quarterly jump since 1994, with CME-implied odds putting 60% probability on four or more additional hikes over the next year. The Journal's own markets desk states it directly: views of permanently low rates "have now been completely cast aside, replaced by new forecasts of an extended period of elevated borrowing costs." A structural combination — AI capex demand for capital, oil near $104/barrel, and mounting government debt issuance — is cited as the reason this is not a blip. A regime prior that dominated portfolio construction for 15 years is being actively dismantled in real time, and the "bonds as safe diversifier" assumption embedded in target-date retirement funds (Vanguard's 2065 fund: ~8% bonds) is the direct casualty.
