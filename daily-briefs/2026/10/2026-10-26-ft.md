## 5. Steel-Man

**Consensus view:** The Chinese luxury slowdown is cyclical — a property-market and stock-market-driven "pause" in demand that recovers once sentiment stabilises, as LVMH's own CFO frames it: "We hope it will subside. We are not sure when."

**Steel-man:** The paper's own reporting undercuts the cyclical framing. Monthly Chinese retail sales growth has averaged under 0.5 per cent for six months — a sharp slowdown versus the prior year, not a blip — and the deterioration is now hitting Hermès, the house analysts considered most insulated from the China cycle because of its scarcity-driven demand model. Bernstein's Luca Solca frames it not as sentiment but as structural: "a prolonged property slump and weak stock market performance have undermined domestic sales over the past few years," compounded by a crackdown on offshore wealth that is making it "tougher" for wealthy Chinese to spend abroad. Kering's own CFO attributes the deceleration "largely" to Chinese clientele with no visibility on timing. Taken together, the drivers — property wealth destruction, a multi-year equity bear market, and tightened capital controls on offshore spending — are policy- and balance-sheet-driven, not emotional, and none of the three is likely to reverse on a quarterly view. The paper's own framing that the industry is "in the grip of a multiyear downturn" is the more defensible read than "pause."

## 6. Prior Under Pressure

**Prior:** Sovereign government debt reliably prices tighter than investment-grade corporate credit — the textbook risk-free-rate hierarchy that underpins most credit and relative-value models.

**Pressure:** The Unhedged data shows this is now false for a meaningful and growing share of the market. French seven-to-ten-year government bonds are trading through BBB-rated corporate paper, and the OECD's broader 1,584-company sample shows the number of issuers with spreads below their own government's benchmark has risen from near zero before 2020 to over 30 in 2026 — a structural, not episodic, shift "across 25 of 1,584 investment-grade companies" at end-2025 alone, concentrated in exactly the jurisdictions (France prominent among them) where fiscal and political risk is rising fastest. The mechanism is intuitive once stated — governments have borrowed heavily while companies kept balance sheets conservative — but it inverts a relationship every desk's relative-value screen still assumes by default.
