## 6. Prior Under Pressure

**Prior:** The Fed's rate path would move toward cuts as inflation cooled, supporting a "soft landing" that eventually re-steepens the curve on falling short rates rather than rising long rates.

**Pressure:** The Fed hiked a quarter point last month and "signaled more to come," the 10-year just hit a 24-year high, and the curve is steepening because the 10-year is rising while the 2-year falls — the opposite of a cuts-led steepening. The inflation driving this is substantially war- and tariff-driven (diesel +72% y/y, gasoline +39% y/y, electricity +3.8% y/y vs. 3.4% headline CPI), which is harder for conventional monetary policy to address and raises the odds the Fed has to choose between credibility (new Chair Warsh is publicly committing to independence amid the Lisa Cook/Powell pressure overhang, per reader correspondence) and growth support. Commercial real estate's mortgage-market distress (11.4% special servicing, retrades becoming routine) is exactly the kind of collateral damage a "higher for longer, and now higher still" scenario produces — not the glide path markets had priced.

## 12. One To Watch

The Electronic Arts bondholder fight over a $1.4 billion alleged default is a small-looking story — a defeasance-structuring dispute — but it is the first concrete test case of what happens when a mega-LBO's debt meets a rate shock and a ratings downgrade simultaneously. EA's sponsors (Saudi Arabia's PIF, Silver Lake, Jared Kushner's Affinity Partners) tried to engineer around a standard change-of-control protection using Treasury defeasance rather than simply paying the 101-cent put; bondholders are fighting back with a formal cooperation agreement and name-brand advisers (Akin Gump, Houlihan Lokey).
