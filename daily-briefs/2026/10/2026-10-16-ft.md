## 1. Today's Themes

**AI infrastructure credit stress is migrating from a niche story to a cross-asset one.** Bonds backing marquee data centre projects — Blue Owl/Meta's "Hyperion" vehicle (below 92 cents from above par in July), Blackstone-owned QTS's "Project Excalibur" (below 88 cents), two Hut 8-developed projects (92-94 cents) — have all broken through par, and CoreWeave's debt has fallen to 75 cents with yields above 15 per cent, a level the market treats as distressed. Gartner's forecast that 80 per cent of "neocloud" providers will cease independent operations by 2030, set against the $8.5tn of AI revenue the same firm says is needed to justify current capex versus the $3.6tn it actually expects to materialise, is the clearest articulation yet of the gap the bond market is now pricing. Cross-reference: this is the same dynamic driving the day's bond rally (10-year Treasury yields fell as much as 15bp to 5.04 per cent) and the Nasdaq's 0.43 per cent slide — investors rotated out of AI-adjacent risk and into sovereign duration in the same session.

**Washington's Ukraine policy has pushed Zelenskyy into open rupture, not just friction.** "The block [on attacking refineries] is out. We are done," he told the FT, directly attacking Trump's diesel-for-sanctions-relief deal with Moscow and the "dangerous" withdrawal of US intelligence and Starlink throttling. This is qualitatively sharper than the February Oval Office clash — Zelenskyy now states plainly that Trump has an "instinctive affinity for autocrats." Cross-reference with the US proposal (reported separately) to move frozen Russian sovereign assets into a Washington-controlled vehicle: European officials rebuffed it outright, and Merz was explicit — "these are assets that are frozen in Europe and they should stay frozen in Europe." Two fronts of the same story: Washington increasingly treating both the war's endgame and its financing as matters it alone controls.

**France's political paralysis has become a banking-sector problem, not just a bond story.** The CAC 40 is underperforming European peers by the widest margin since 2010; Société Générale is down more than 20 per cent since August and BNP Paribas 19 per cent, both on balance-sheet exposure to sovereign debt as 10-year OAT yields approach a quarter-century high. Goldman Sachs attributes roughly half of France's equity underperformance to a standalone "French risk premium" distinct from sector effects (luxury, autos).

**A Middle East proxy conflict is dragging in a reluctant third party with no good options.** Houthi missile strikes on Saudi Arabia's King Khalid airport have triggered Riyadh's mutual defence pact with Pakistan, pulling Islamabad toward a war domestically unpopular and diplomatically costly — it also mediates between Iran and the US. [WEAK SIGNAL]: Pakistan's "financial dependence on the Saudi state" (a $3bn emergency bailout in April, a $5bn central-bank deposit extension) means this is a dependency relationship being tested by security demands, not just a market one — a pattern worth watching if other Gulf-dependent borrowers face similar asks.

**Germany's external assertiveness is outrunning its domestic mandate.** Merz pledges to "widen the toolbox" on EU trade defence and floats a Franco-German "trade weapon" against coercion, even as the AfD polls near 30 per cent nationally and the CDU lost a regional parliamentary seat for the first time in its 81-year history. The disconnect between ambitious external posture and eroding domestic position is a structural vulnerability, not a messaging problem.

## 2. Finance & Markets

Rates led: the 10-year Treasury yield fell as much as 15bp to a three-week low of 5.04 per cent (later 5.09 per cent) as a "months-long bond sell-off shifted into reverse," per Vanguard's Ales Koutny, who noted the move came despite hotter-than-expected US inflation data — "the risk-off move has trumped all of it." German Bunds followed, closing 6bp lower at 3.41 per cent. This is a flight-to-quality move triggered by AI-linked credit stress, not a macro data signal, and the two should not be read as agreeing with each other.

Credit is the story beneath the rates move. CoreWeave bonds at 75 cents on the dollar (yields above 15 per cent) and a cluster of special-purpose-vehicle debt backing Meta, Microsoft, Nvidia and Oracle tenancies sliding below par across the board — Allianz's Ludovic Subran's framing, "data centre bonds are having their Darwin moment, where the weakest credits struggle to survive," is the apt one: this reads as a differentiation event within AI credit, not (yet) a systemic one, since Nvidia itself still shed over $300bn in market value this week on sentiment rather than fundamentals.

Separately, a draft EU-UK deal would extend "Made in Europe" procurement access to UK carmakers only if London aligns its China-tariff regime with Brussels — a concrete, near-term bind for PM Andy Burnham's government, since it simultaneously requires stricter foreign-investment screening. [FT ANGLE]: the piece frames this as a negotiating assertion of EU regulatory sovereignty over a non-member state's trade policy, a framing a US outlet would likely skip in favour of the UK-domestic political angle.

On sanctions: the US has proposed moving some of the ~€260bn in frozen Russian sovereign assets into a special-purpose vehicle "overseen by Washington" to revive peace talks — floated by Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner in Miami talks with Ukraine, France, Germany, the UK, the EU and Nato. European delegates "rebuffed" the idea; Merz was blunt that control stays in Europe. [FT ANGLE]: the FT frames this primarily as a sovereignty and control dispute between Washington and Brussels — distinct from how a US outlet would likely frame the same proposal as a negotiating tactic to end the war faster.

**Institutional and structural dynamics.** The Zelenskyy-Trump rupture is the dominant structural story: Washington no longer provides financial aid to Ukraine (only paid-for weapons), intelligence sharing was reportedly conditioned on Kyiv halting refinery strikes, and Starlink speed was throttled — three distinct levers of leverage being used simultaneously. NATO's Mark Rutte confirmed the alliance will "adjust deterrence and defence posture" after suspected Russian drone strikes set two tankers ablaze in Romanian territorial waters near the Ukraine border — a Black Sea/NATO-territory escalation that received comparatively modest play relative to its significance. Separately, Norway's $2.3tn sovereign fund is reviewing its ban on investing in nuclear-weapons producers (Lockheed Martin, Airbus, Boeing, BAE Systems) — a decision former Nato secretary-general Jens Stoltenberg once called a "paradox," now live again as his government depends on left-wing parties who call lifting the ban "absolutely insane."

## 6. Prior Under Pressure

**Prior:** Core Eurozone sovereigns (France chief among them) are structurally insulated from the kind of bond-market discipline typically reserved for emerging markets or peripheral Europe — "bond vigilante" dynamics are an EM/Italy/Greece phenomenon, not a France one.

**Pressure:** French 10-year yields are "nearly 5 per cent, their highest in almost a quarter of a century," the CAC 40 is underperforming European peers by the widest margin since 2010, and French banks — not just the sovereign curve — are down 19-20 per cent since August on direct government-debt balance-sheet exposure. Goldman Sachs attributes roughly half of the equity underperformance specifically to a "French risk premium" independent of sector composition. A G7 founding member with the Eurozone's second-largest economy is now trading with the equity- and bank-sector symptoms of a sovereign-risk event, with an unresolved budget fight and a hostile-to-markets presidential frontrunner (Le Pen) on the 2027 horizon. This is a materially harder prior to hold with the same confidence it enjoyed even six months ago.
