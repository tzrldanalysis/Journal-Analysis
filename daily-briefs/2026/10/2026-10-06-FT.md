## 6. Prior Under Pressure

**Prior:** France, as a Eurozone "core" economy, enjoys a structurally lower cost of capital than periphery states (Italy, Greece, Spain) by virtue of size, institutional credibility and historically deep, liquid debt markets.

**Pressure:** It now costs France more to borrow than Italy or Greece — a reversal the Big Read states explicitly, noting French yields have risen "the most out of any G7 bond market" since the Iran war began and that French borrowing costs passed Greece's in late 2024 and Italy's last year. More than a quarter of investment-grade French corporates (LVMH, Sanofi) borrow more cheaply than their own government. The core-periphery hierarchy that has organised Eurozone risk pricing since 2012 is being quietly inverted in real time, not by a French default but by a market simply repricing French political/fiscal risk above what used to be considered the "periphery."

## 12. One To Watch

The ECB's Transmission Protection Instrument — an emergency bond-buying tool created in 2023, never once used, and now the thing multiple serious people in this edition are quietly betting will have to be deployed on France, a G7 "core" economy, for the first time ever. It received a few paragraphs buried in the Big Read and a glancing mention in Bini Smaghi's QT column, yet it is the single mechanism standing between "contained French political story" and "Eurozone-wide crisis" if Lecornu's government falls or an OAT auction goes badly during the live budget fight. Every other story in this edition — Japan's bond stress, the euro's slide, hedge funds chasing AI-uncorrelated diversification — becomes more or less dangerous depending on whether this specific, untested tool actually works when called upon. It deserves far more scrutiny than it is currently getting.
