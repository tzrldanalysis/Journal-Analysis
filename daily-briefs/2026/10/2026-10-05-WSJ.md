## 9. Europe Watch

France's 10-year OAT-Bund spread reached 1.55 percentage points on Friday, within range of the 1.81-point peak set during the 2011 eurozone sovereign crisis, as a poorly received budget from Emmanuel Macron's government collided with a widening Marine Le Pen polling lead for the 2027 presidential race. The move was amplified by a wholesale unwind of "long France/short Germany" carry and swap-spread trades that hedge funds and real-money managers (Pimco's Konstantin Veit, L&G's John Roe) had piled into over the summer on the assumption French risk was overpriced relative to default probability. Short-dated French yields swung as much as 0.40 percentage points in two days — among the largest moves "in years," per SMBC's Hank Calenti — before stabilizing somewhat Friday as French stocks and the euro recovered. More than half of France's roughly $3.9 trillion government-debt stock is foreign-owned (MUFG), which cuts both ways: it deepened the market but also means outflows can be fast. Credit-ratings agencies could move if Le Pen's fiscal platform (including reversing pension-age increases) solidifies as the front-runner heading into 2027 — a scenario analysts say could "worsen France's public finances" further. Separately, Germany's Merz pledged over €1.3 billion in new Ukraine aid (including long-range strike weapons) during a Kyiv visit, underscoring a diverging transatlantic defense posture from Washington's more transactional approach elsewhere.
