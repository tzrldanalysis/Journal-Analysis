**Trade, tariffs, sanctions.** The EU and China produced a "pragmatic and productive" joint statement: China agreed to limit hybrid-car exports to the EU (relief for European carmakers) in exchange for "further discussions" on power inverters, after the EU barred its own funds from financing projects using Chinese, Iranian, Russian or North Korean inverters. Separately, the UK, France, Canada, Spain and the Netherlands have announced or imposed sanctions on goods from Israeli West Bank settlements, a mechanism-level escalation (goods-origin sanctions, not diplomatic statements) that the US has not joined.

## 5. Steel-Man

**Steel-man:** Robert Armstrong's own numbers in this edition undercut that reading: the 30 largest AI capital spenders will spend over $200bn this year against projected negative free cash flow of at least $54bn, meaning the boom is substantially financed by $466bn of new AI-linked debt and by American households' equity exposure (stocks are almost a third of household assets) rather than by hyperscaler earnings alone. NextDC's 44 per cent share collapse on a cut earnings forecast, AirTrunk's paused Singapore listing, and Goodman Group's abandoned Sydney data-centre plan — all in today's edition — show local capacity, grid and political constraints already biting in the real world, not just in financing structures. If the pattern generalises beyond Australia, the "self-funding supercycle" framing looks closer to a leveraged bet that savers, not operators, are underwriting.
