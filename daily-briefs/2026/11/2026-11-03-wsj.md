## 5. Steel-Man

**Consensus view:** The AI capex supercycle is entering a bubble-deflation phase, and this week's capital-markets action — the Nasdaq's worst month since March 2025, the equal-weight-vs-cap-weight divergence last seen in 2009, Anthropic's valuation cut, and the joint Marvell/Lumentum selloff — confirms that investors are correctly de-rating AI infrastructure risk.

**Steel-man:** Palantir's commercial revenue grew 148% and surpassed government revenue for the first time, with Karp explicitly describing a shift of enterprise demand toward controlled AI deployment rather than retreat from it. Anthropic's own disclosed numbers show quarterly revenue jumping from $11.6 billion to $17 billion — the kind of growth that, even net of $600 million in price cuts, looks like improving unit economics and intensifying demand, not demand destruction; a valuation cut from $2 trillion chatter to $1.5–1.6 trillion still implies extraordinary growth is priced in, not a bubble bursting. JPMorgan's own research, cited in the same Markets section that frames this as an AI unwind, argues legislative gridlock — the most likely midterm outcome — has historically coincided with stronger-than-average equity returns (7.7%). Meanwhile the ISM manufacturing index expanded for a 10th straight month (55.5, the strongest production reading since 2021) and corporate results from Marriott, Estée Lauder and Fox all beat expectations. The more defensible read is that this week's mega-cap selloff is substantially midterm-related positioning (option hedging ahead of Nov. 4, debt issuers deliberately waiting out election uncertainty) layered on top of a genuine but narrower re-rating of financing-dependent AI infrastructure plays like CoreWeave — not evidence of a broad AI-demand bubble popping.
