## 5. Steel-Man

The strongest case **against** the "AI bubble" framing (Foroohar's column notwithstanding): Anthropic's willingness to commit $3.4bn to physical power infrastructure — a multi-year, hard-to-reverse capital commitment — is itself evidence that at least one major lab's own internal demand forecasting is robust enough to justify locking in gas-turbine supply years in advance. Bubbles are rarely accompanied by this kind of unglamorous, long-duration industrial commitment; speculative manias concentrate capital in liquid, reversible bets, not turbine orders with multi-year lead times. If Anthropic's revenue trajectory disappoints, the turbine order is a sunk cost that argues the company's own forecasts — not just investor sentiment — were wrong, which is a different and arguably more serious claim than "the market got overexcited."

## 7. What Would Have To Be True

**For the AI-bubble thesis (Foroohar) to resolve bearishly within the next 12 months:** AI lab revenue growth would have to visibly decelerate while capex commitments (like Anthropic's $3.4bn turbine order) remain locked in, producing a free-cash-flow squeeze that forces either equity raises at depressed valuations or write-downs on power/compute assets. The local political backlash would need to translate into actual project cancellations or multi-year permitting delays, not just noisy local votes, to meaningfully slow the physical buildout.

**For the UK "foreign sell-off" trend (FT Big Read) to reverse:** UK equity valuations would need to re-rate meaningfully relative to US and European peers — plausibly requiring either a sustained sterling devaluation that makes UK assets cheap enough to attract domestic bidders back into competition, or a policy intervention (e.g., pension-fund allocation mandates, stamp-duty reform on UK shares) that changes the structural demand-side disadvantage UK equities face versus foreign strategic acquirers.
