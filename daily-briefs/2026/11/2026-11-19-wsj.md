## 3. Geopolitics & Policy

**Trade/tariffs/sanctions mechanics.** The Commerce Department this week placed a new export-licensing requirement on sales of Nvidia-chip-related equipment to Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Singapore, aimed at closing loopholes that let Chinese firms access US-chip compute abroad; companies have ~60 days to comment. The administration is also weighing a rule requiring US companies to disclose which Chinese AI models they deploy, and new restrictions on Chinese equipment in US data centers. Market implication: constrains Nvidia's addressable market incrementally but Kress says demand "far exceeding supply" should absorb it; the geopolitical signal is sharper — this is the first substantive hardening of the "light-touch" China-tech detente the administration had maintained for most of the past year, catalyzed by a personal scandal rather than a policy debate. This is a structural signal, not a one-off: China is using soft-power staging to contest the narrative of technological leadership at the exact moment its hard levers (chip access, rare-earth export controls) are being met with harder US controls. Iran's expansion of strikes to Kuwait and Bahrain — the first attack on Kuwait in over two months — alongside intensifying ethnic-insurgency attacks in Iran's Kurdish and Baloch regions, signals a regime "chipping away" at its own capacity to project power even as it escalates externally; the US has built up to three carrier groups in the region (Theodore Roosevelt, George Washington, George H.W.
