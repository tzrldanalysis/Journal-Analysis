## 3. Geopolitics & Policy

**Trade/tariffs/sanctions mechanics.** The Russian diesel waiver is the cleanest test case in today's edition of whether Trump's trade mechanics deliver as advertised. Mechanism: Treasury temporarily waived sanctions on October 9 to allow Russian diesel exports, sold publicly as a route to lower U.S. fuel prices. Scope/effect: Russia's diesel and gasoil exports rose 165% month-over-month, generating $1.9 billion for Moscow since the waiver, while U.S. retail diesel prices averaged $6.25 from October 12 to November 9 versus $6.20 the week the deal was announced — essentially flat to slightly higher. Market implication: no consumer benefit materialized; geopolitical signal: the waiver reads as unilateral concession to Putin with no reciprocal leverage gained, reassuring the Kremlin it can keep rejecting peace terms while funding its war effort.

The aluminum tariff mechanism (50% rate, in force, driving a record-plus-75% domestic premium) is generating enough of its own price distortion that the CFTC is now exploring a parallel domestic futures contract — a tariff creating new market infrastructure rather than simply redirecting trade flows, with direct cost implications for U.S. aluminum-consuming manufacturers.

The China-vape carve-out (unilateral non-enforcement rather than a formal rate change, tied to Trump campaign manager Chris LaCivita's advocacy for importer Hillfield, which paid $30 million in tariffs on detained Geek Bar shipments) is a second instance this week of a politically connected actor securing favorable treatment on Chinese goods — directly paralleling the Nvidia chip-smuggling dynamic and raising the same question about whether stated enforcement priorities survive contact with administration allies.

Chip-export mechanics: Commerce is drafting rules to add Malaysia, Indonesia and Singapore to the export-restricted country list for advanced chips like Nvidia's Blackwell series, which would require export licenses prohibiting Chinese remote access to the computing power. This directly threatens the $34 billion-plus Southeast Asian data-center buildout (BlackRock-backed Vena Group, Alibaba-linked DayOne, Aolani's ByteDance relationship) that grew up specifically to route around earlier controls.

**Institutional/structural dynamics.** China's maritime pressure on the Philippines around the South China Sea is accelerating on a measurable curve — Acled counts 55 incidents January-mid-April, 94 mid-April-late-July, 127 late-July-October — timed precisely to a period when U.S. carrier and military assets have shifted toward the Middle East for the Iran war.
