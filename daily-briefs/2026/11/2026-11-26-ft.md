## 3. Geopolitics & Policy

**Trade/tariffs mechanics.** The US has proposed tariffs of up to 20 per cent on specific goods lists from more than a dozen partners. Mechanism: rates generally match those already agreed in prior trade deals, with the higher of the two applicable tariffs taking precedence where categories overlap; China's new rate could double from 20 to 40 per cent if the US judges Beijing non-compliant with its rare-earth export pledges; the EU, Japan and South Korea face 15 per cent (the EU's unchanged from its existing deal); Singapore 10 per cent; Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand and Bangladesh were proposed at 18 per cent. Market implication: this is a largely codifying move (matching existing deal rates) rather than a fresh escalation for most partners, which should limit the equity-market impact versus the headline framing — yet Deutsche Bank still cited "the latest tariff headlines" as a driver of the day's broader risk-off tone. Geopolitical signal: the administration is treating tariff policy as fully insulated from the midterm verdict that voters delivered explicitly over "high prices, driven by tariffs and the Iran war" — a structural statement about where trade policy now sits relative to electoral accountability in the current term.
