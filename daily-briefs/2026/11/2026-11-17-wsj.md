## 3. Geopolitics & Policy

**Trade, tariffs and export controls (first priority).** The Commerce Department issued a rule Monday restricting chip shipments and remote compute access to Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, Singapore and Brazil, effective immediately with a 60-day comment window. The rule was written by Treasury and the National Security Council rather than the Bureau of Industry and Security — an unprecedented bypass aimed at closing the "backdoor" loophole exploited by Singapore-based Megaspeed to divert Nvidia chips into China. Market implication: AMD and other data-center names fell roughly 5% on the announcement, and the rule signals the Bessent/Rubio hawkish faction is currently winning the internal fight against Commerce Secretary Lutnick's more accommodating posture, raising the probability of further escalation (including Entity List additions) ahead of Thursday's summit. Geopolitical signal: Washington is now treating data-center geography, not just chip serial numbers, as the export-control perimeter — a structural tightening rather than a one-off gesture.

The Nvidia espionage scandal and Thursday's Trump-Xi-Putin trilateral in Shenzhen — the first summit of its kind, excluding Ukraine and the EU entirely — compound the same dynamic. Market implication: any perceived softening of US China posture at the summit would read bullish for Nvidia/AMD China exposure; a harder line (consistent with Monday's rule) would compound existing chip-sector stress. Geopolitical signal: China is using the optics to project convening power between two "weakened" rivals — Russia bogged down in Ukraine and a US president one Italian senator called a "lame duck" — while Ukraine's foreign minister frames the summit instead as "Munich 1938," a sacrifice of Ukrainian and European interests by the great powers.

**Institutional and structural dynamics (second priority).** The blue wave's reshaping of state-level AI-infrastructure geography is itself an institutional shift — state government has become an active veto point on AI capex, with capital likely to concentrate further toward geographically diversified or "friendly" pipelines (Amazon's 17.6 gigawatts, Google's multistate footprint) and away from politically concentrated bets like OpenAI's single Ohio project. In Europe, the European Commission's approval of UniCredit's takeover of Commerzbank — overriding Berlin's earlier political rejection — is a live test of whether EU institutions can override national banking protectionism; competition chief Teresa Ribera said the EU would "react accordingly" against any government trying to block it. Russia's hybrid sabotage campaign against NATO's Baltic flank and France's domestic fragility both read as tests of Western alliance cohesion at precisely the moment Putin is calculating that Western fatigue strengthens his hand.
