## 1. Today's Themes

**The AI-capex rally that "resumed" Wednesday is being financed by increasingly circular and distressed credit, not fresh capital.** CoreWeave jumped 25% on a Q3 beat and dragged the chip complex with it (Micron +12%, Intel +7.5%, AMD +7.3%, Broadcom +4%), but CoreWeave's own CEO conceded on the earnings call that the company "will not use the unsecured debt market to fund our business at current yields" — its most-traded 2032 bond (9.625% coupon) is yielding roughly 15%. The $39 billion of 2026 capex is instead being funded through customer prepayments (~$9 billion expected over the next three months alone) and vendor financing, while data-center landlords now require investment-grade rental backstops before accepting CoreWeave as a tenant. Simultaneously, TSMC approved up to $500 million each into Anthropic and OpenAI — the same companies whose chip orders drive TSMC's own revenue, which is itself up 60% year-over-year. The rally and the financing structure underneath it are not the same story.

**Nvidia's governance crisis is now inseparable from AI-sentiment risk, and the market is pricing them as if they're not.** Nvidia's board is reportedly preparing to oust Jensen Huang over two affairs — one with Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, whom U.S. intelligence officials believe is a Chinese intelligence asset, and a second with Nvidia's own healthcare-unit executive, Kimberly Powell. The stock still rose 2.9% intraday Wednesday on the broader AI rally before falling roughly 8% after hours on the ouster/second-affair reporting. The Justice Department is running multiple investigations tied to Alice Huang and Senate Democrats (Warren, Coons) are demanding document preservation on Nvidia's China chip-export history — this is a live legal and leadership overhang sitting underneath the world's most important AI stock.

**Core inflation is reaccelerating on a supply shock the Fed cannot easily reverse.** Core CPI ran at a 3.7% annualized pace over the three months through October, up sharply from 1.6% in the three months through July. Fed governor Waller is open to a 50-basis-point December hike; Dallas Fed's Logan hinted at a faster pace; Philadelphia's Paulson urged patience. Crucially, Waller's own explanation is that diesel prices remain near $6.10/gallon despite crude falling more than 20% this month, because war-damaged refining capacity "isn't going to come back soon" — a supply constraint rate hikes don't fix. The 2-year Treasury yield's 0.18-point jump Tuesday was the largest since April 9, 2025 (the day of Trump's tariff pause), signaling a real repricing of Fed expectations.

**The Iran negotiating channel is narrowing to a single, politically exposed figure just as the regime's assassination campaign against U.S. officials continues.** Vance is emerging as lead negotiator, having sent Iran's hardline security chief Mohsen Rezaei a letter requesting direct talks, with Doha floated as a venue. But Treasury Secretary Bessent has been relocated to a military base over Iran-linked threats on his life, the FBI just added a $20 million bounty for the IRGC operative who allegedly plotted to kill John Bolton, and Middle East officials worry IRGC commander Ahmed Vahidi — more hawkish than Rezaei — could override any deal the way a previous Iranian interlocutor (Ghalibaf) was sidelined after conciliatory talks in Islamabad.

**[WEAK SIGNAL]** Crypto market infrastructure is quietly pricing and wrapping real-world equity risk ahead of, or parallel to, formal capital-markets pricing. Hyperliquid perpetual-futures trading implies an Anthropic post-IPO market cap of $1.8 trillion — roughly 35% above the $1.33 trillion valuation Anthropic itself is targeting in its roadshow. Separately, Nasdaq-backed Payward (Kraken) launched tokenized trading of more than 200 UK/European blue-chip stocks (Siemens, Shell, LVMH, the full FTSE 100 and DAX) without needing issuer consent in Europe. Neither story has its own headline yet, but both point to a shadow pricing/settlement layer for equity risk forming alongside — not fully reconciled with — the regulated market.

Market implication: tariff-driven cost inflation on durable goods is still working through the system via enforcement actions, not just headline rates — a slow-burn CPI input. Geopolitical signal: reshoring remains economically unworkable even with tariff protection (Krinsky's High Point, N.C. plant lost $3 million in ten months before closing), undercutting the administration's core trade-policy premise.

**Institutional/structural dynamics.** The Vance-Rezaei channel (first priority) is the dominant story: a proposed confidence-building exchange — U.S. carrier pullback and partial port-blockade easing in return for 25 tankers transiting Hormuz — is real but fragile, with Iranian diplomats privately skeptical and hardliner Vahidi a wildcard who could override Rezaei's position. Market implication: any blockade-easing headline should be read against this override risk, not taken as de-escalation on arrival. Second-order: the global ballistic-missile proliferation story (cheap, precise missiles now in Houthi and Iranian hands, outpacing Western interceptor production by Lockheed Martin and the not-yet-operational Franco-Italian Samp/T consortium) is forcing a rethink of forward-basing doctrine that predates this administration and will outlast the Iran war specifically — Ukraine's air-defense shortage is the same supply problem in a different theater.
