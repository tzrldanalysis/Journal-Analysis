## 1. Today's Themes

**Markets are aggressively unwinding the "blue wave kills AI capex" selloff, even as Nvidia's succession and espionage risk reshuffles AI-trade leadership.** The S&P 500 rose 2.3% to 7549.94, the Nasdaq 2.7% to 25253.14, and the Dow 2.2% (1,104 points) to 52389.75 — the S&P's best day in seven months, with all 11 sectors closing higher for the first time since May. Nvidia itself was the exception, falling 4.3% to $178.67 after its board launched an investigation into CEO Jensen Huang's affair with a suspected Chinese intelligence operative, while direct competitors and customers — AMD (+13%), Broadcom, CoreWeave (+5%), Nebius (+9%), and hyperscalers Amazon, Microsoft, Meta and Alphabet (all +1% to +5%) — rallied. This is capital rotating within the AI trade, not fleeing it.

**The Fed is still hiking on inflation, not cutting on growth — a hawkish bias that collides head-on with the midterm "affordability" mandate.** Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee said inflation remains "the big problem," not employment, days after the Fed raised its target rate a quarter point in September to 3.75–4.00%, with markets pricing a further hike next month. The 10-year Treasury yields 4.863% and the 2-year 4.545%. Democrats swept into power on promises to lower costs, cap data-center electricity rates, and expand healthcare — all while the rate-setting authority that actually controls financing costs remains in tightening mode under Trump appointees through 2029-2030.

**Iran's war is generating spillover damage inside NATO territory, and the oil market is building physical hedges against an undefined endpoint.** France and the U.S. formally attributed cyberattacks on supermarket refrigeration and blood-bank systems across six-plus European countries to Iran's IRGC-linked Shahid Kaveh unit; Paris is seeking international arrest warrants but no EU sanctions have been imposed. Separately, Gulf producers (Aramco's Jurong Rock Caverns deal, Adnoc's Fujairah and India storage) and importing nations (Japan's $10B financing framework, Australia's $2.1B diesel stockpile) are racing to rebuild inventories after the U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve fell to its lowest since 1982 — a structural response to a war whose resolution nobody can price.

**Asia's AI-linked equity markets are in a far deeper, more durable drawdown than Monday's U.S. rebound suggests.** South Korea's Kospi is down 45% from its June peak, Japan's Nikkei Semiconductor index has fallen 54% from its high, and Kioxia fell nearly 37% last week alone, even as authorities in Taiwan, South Korea and Japan intervene with short-selling curbs and stabilization funds. Cross-reference: this directly complicates theme one — the U.S. snapback and the Asian AI-supply-chain rout are happening simultaneously, in the same global AI-capex complex, which argues against reading Monday's U.S. rally as evidence the capex-fear trade is over everywhere.

**Nvidia's governance model — and the "benign dictator" bargain investors have struck with founder-controlled tech — is being tested in real time.** The Journal's own Streetwise column notes Nvidia's board has disclosed no public waiver for Huang's personal relationship under its own conflict-of-interest code, even as the company's proxy statement explicitly warns that lack of succession planning could "hinder" strategic execution. Huang's personally-brokered relationships with Trump, Macron, Merz and TSMC's Morris Chang are the kind of soft power that cannot be inherited by a successor.

**[WEAK SIGNAL] AI is quietly industrializing low-skill cyberattacks on physical infrastructure.** The refrigerator/blood-bank hacks are explicitly described as enabled by AI-automated scanning for exposed, poorly secured IoT devices — a capability shift cybersecurity specialists say "removes the barriers" that used to require "specialist skills, time and money." It hasn't yet produced a story of its own beyond the Iran attribution, but it rhymes with warnings from CISA, the U.K.'s NCSC and Palo Alto Networks about vulnerable grid-adjacent devices (solar inverters, vending machines, DVRs) — a dry run for something larger if the Iran war escalates.
