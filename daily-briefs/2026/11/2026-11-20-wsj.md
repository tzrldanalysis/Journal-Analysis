## 3. Geopolitics & Policy

**Trade, tariffs, and sanctions mechanics.** The Commerce Department closed a loophole that let Chinese AI firms access Nvidia's most powerful chips remotely through data centers in Southeast Asia, adding Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, Singapore and Brazil to the list of countries subject to export restrictions. The mechanism specifically targets firms like Megaspeed, which built offshore data centers to route billions of dollars of Nvidia chips into China. Market implication: Alibaba fell roughly 10% this week, and DeepSeek and Moonshot AI have both throttled their chatbots under compute shortages. Geopolitical signal: Washington is now asserting jurisdiction over third countries' sovereign data-center access as an extension of chip-export enforcement — a meaningful escalation in the extraterritorial reach of U.S. tech controls that risks friction with ASEAN governments and Brazil who had no direct role in the underlying dispute.

Separately, the U.S. temporarily lifted sanctions on Russian diesel exports specifically to relieve a tight global fuel market — sanctions relief deployed as a price tool even before a single compliant cargo has reached American shores. The retaliation risk here is asymmetric: it is Washington loosening enforcement, not an adversary escalating, which makes it a rare case of sanctions policy being used proactively to cool inflation rather than punish behavior.

**Institutional and structural dynamics.** The Shenzhen summit's substance was thin by design — a joint statement on nuclear risk, a pledge to boost refined-petroleum supply, calls to rein in ICC "overreach," and vague UN-reform language, all functioning more as a shared "rewrite the old order" signal than as policy. Structurally, the format itself matters more than the content: a first-ever U.S.-China-Russia trilateral legitimizes a "G3" framing of global order that both flatters Xi (equal footing with the U.S. president for the third time this year) and rehabilitates Putin (sharing a stage five years after becoming a pariah) while sidelining the European officials and Ukrainians who actually have the most at stake. That exclusion is compounding, not incidental: Kushner's parallel, quieter NATO-for-territory channel with Zelensky and European security advisers is happening in Dublin and the UAE, outside the summit, suggesting the real negotiating track and the real diplomatic theater are now two separate processes.

The Denmark-Greenland-U.S. trilateral security deal, approved by the Danish Parliament, resolves — for now — an intra-NATO rift that the Trump administration itself created through its push for a U.S. takeover of Greenland. The deal trades expanded U.S. basing rights for a step back from annexation rhetoric, a face-saving structure that eases tension with the EU and NATO without requiring Washington to formally abandon its stated interest in the territory.

The ruling doesn't bind that separate case, but it changes the evidentiary and rhetorical landscape in which it will eventually be decided.

## 6. Prior Under Pressure

**Prior:** AI hyperscaler and frontier-lab capex is effectively self-funding and low-credit-risk, because the balance sheets standing behind it — Nvidia, the biggest private-credit and alternative-asset managers, sovereign-scale venture capital — are simply too large to let financing gaps matter.

**Pressure:** Nvidia's own $500 billion "financing consortium" with Apollo, Blackstone, BlackRock, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR has deployed not one dollar months after its August announcement. Huang's 25% residual-value guarantee cap sits well below the 75-85% Broadcom offered on its own comparable $35 billion and $63 billion Anthropic financings, and lenders are now demanding guarantees above 25% at the exact moment Nvidia — under a more cautious CFO-turned-interim-CEO — is retreating rather than expanding its commitments. CoreWeave, the clearest beneficiary of Nvidia's prior largesse, fell 6.1% the same day on explicit credit-position fears, and $820 billion has been erased across just eight Nvidia-linked equities since Oct. 6. Combined, the prior that financing will simply materialize to fund the buildout is now resting on a counterparty that is visibly tightening its terms, not loosening them, and on a guarantor whose own CEO just resigned in scandal.
