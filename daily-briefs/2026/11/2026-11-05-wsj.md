## 3. Geopolitics & Policy

**Trade, tariffs, and sanctions mechanics.** No new tariff action was announced today, but the mechanism to watch is structural: Trump retains independent executive authority over most sanctions and tariff tools regardless of congressional control, since — per the A7 analysis — "sanctions laws" and "implementation" remain "within the remit of the executive branch." Expect continued pressure on Asian allies (Japan, South Korea) over hundreds of billions in committed U.S. investment, and a possible reigniting of the EU trade fight, even without a GOP majority to legislate around. Separately, Israel aid faces a real mechanical constraint: Trump said Tuesday he plans to end U.S. military aid to Israel, but the U.S. has already structured roughly $7 billion in pledged annual aid through 2028 and Israel has ordered an estimated $10 billion beyond what's currently funded (A8). Current and former officials call an abrupt cutoff "not plausible" this year given funding already committed by Congress — the mechanism (Foreign Military Financing's forward-purchasing structure) makes unwinding a multi-year process regardless of political will.

**Institutional and structural dynamics.** The clearest second-order signal is the Brent crude selloff (-8.7% to $94.62) — markets are pricing a real probability that the Trump administration pursues a negotiated Hormuz deal over further Iran strikes now that "war funding and appointees" are "subject to a hostile majority," per Rystad Energy's Claudio Galimberti. But the Pentagon has, per the Journal's own reporting, been "preparing to resume major combat operations for weeks," moving a third carrier strike group and up to 10,000 more troops toward the region — the de-escalation bet is not unanimous within the administration. China's read is notably confident: Fudan University's Wu Xinbo said Beijing will "look beyond Trump" now and "continue to play hardball," feeling emboldened by the Democratic sweep even though trade authority stays with the executive branch. Separately, Saudi Arabia faces a structural squeeze: the Houthis are demanding $51 billion to end hostilities after seizing territory near the Bab al-Mandeb chokepoint, with Riyadh's traditional ally (the UAE) having withdrawn from Yemen entirely and Turkey/Pakistan reluctant to formalize support through the new Mecca Pact (A8). In Europe, NATO allies are "ramping up military spending and preparing for the U.S. to play a smaller role," per an EU Institute for Security Studies analyst, after Trump questioned U.S. NATO membership — a structural shift in burden-sharing now compounding whatever happens with the Iran de-escalation bet.
