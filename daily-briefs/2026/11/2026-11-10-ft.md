**SoftBank's leverage is now visibly straining at the point where falling collateral values and tightening credit conditions meet — on the day it reports half-year earnings.** Arm and SoftBank's other listed holdings have fallen roughly 60% since June; the loan-to-value ratio on pledged Arm shares has risen to 18–22% from 13% in June against a 25% self-imposed ceiling; five-year CDS spreads have widened sharply; and SB Energy's planned $50bn-valuation listing may be delayed, which has knock-on consequences for OpenAI's own IPO timing (SB Energy is "the final customer" for large swaths of SB Energy's power plans, and SoftBank's stake-size and funding commitments to OpenAI and Anthropic are predicated on continued access to capital markets).

**[WEAK SIGNAL] AI-automated scanning for exposed IoT and critical-infrastructure devices is spreading faster than the diplomatic and sanctions response can track it.** France has formally attributed cyberattacks on supermarket refrigeration and blood-bank systems to Iran's IRGC-linked Shahid Kaveh unit; the campaign, which began with 700+ French retail sites on October 11–12, has since spread to Sweden, Finland, Estonia, Belgium and Ireland, with Paris seeking international arrest warrants while an EU official confirmed Monday there is "no immediate plan" for sanctions. ## 6. Prior Under Pressure

**Prior:** Founder- or CEO-concentrated governance (no chair, no clear succession plan, outsized personal authority) is a tolerable, even value-additive, feature of hypergrowth technology companies, because it preserves decisive, fast-moving leadership through a capital-intensive buildout phase.

**Pressure:** The FT View states this plainly — Nvidia's flat, chairless structure with 60-plus direct reports "paradoxically concentrate[s] power at the top," making Huang "a uniquely juicy target for nefarious actors wanting to bypass protections that his employees take as read." SpaceX's own IPO prospectus admits it could not find a Musk replacement "in a timely manner or at all." Anthropic, which markets itself as "a research lab," is simultaneously racing to own data centres and pay SpaceX billions for compute — the same concentration-of-control dynamic, with the same absence of a stated succession plan. The prior holds only if none of these companies experiences a Huang-style idiosyncratic shock; today's edition supplies the first live counter-example at index-moving scale (Nvidia is ~7% of the S&P 500).
